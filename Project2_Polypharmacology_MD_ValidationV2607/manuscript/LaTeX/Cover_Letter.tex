\noindent Dear Prof.\\ Merz,

We submit our manuscript \textbf{``Resistance-Resilient Polypharmacological Antimalarials from African Natural Products: Molecular Dynamics Validation Against Resistance Mutants''} for consideration as an Article in \textit{JCIM}. This work presents the first systematic molecular dynamics validation of antimalarial leads against a panel of African-prevalent resistance mutations, integrating \SI{30000}{\nano\second} of simulation across 220 protein--ligand systems with three novel scoring metrics.

\textbf{Central finding: Resistance-aware lead validation.} We validate the 20 highest-ranked polypharmacological candidates from our generative library against six clinically relevant resistance mutations (PfDHFR-N51I, C59R, S108N, I164L; PfCRT-K76T, K76A) using \SI{30000}{\nano\second} of MD simulation and MM-GBSA free energy calculations. Three novel metrics are introduced: the Resistance Resilience Score (RRS), which classifies compounds by their ability to maintain binding affinity across mutant variants; the African Chemical Space Index (ACSI), which quantifies the degree to which generated compounds preserve the structural character of African natural products; and the Polypharmacology Network Score (PNS), which weights multi-target engagement by target centrality in the \textit{P. falciparum} interactome. External validation via the Tartarus docking benchmark confirms that MPO drug-likeness scores are orthogonal to binding affinity (Spearman $\rho \approx 0$, $p > 0.7$), motivating the combined MPO + docking ranking strategy. Comprehensive analysis confirms successful NPT equilibration for all four systems. Temperature stability within \SI{0.5}{\kelvin} and pressure convergence within \SI{10}{\bar} were verified for the three locally analysed targets (PfClpP, PfCRT, PfDHFR), with stable backbone RMSD after periodic boundary correction (mean RMSD: PfClpP \SI{0.215}{\nano\metre}, PfCRT \SI{0.262}{\nano\metre}, PfDHFR \SI{0.343}{\nano\metre}). The fourth complex (PfATP4) underwent successful Berendsen-barostat NPT equilibration on its single-chain topology, and is proceeding to production MD on the HPC cluster.

\textbf{Scientific contribution.} This work establishes a resistance-aware computational validation framework through three interconnected components:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Resistance Resilience Score (RRS):} A five-tier classification (Class A* through D) jointly accounting for binding potency and resistance resilience, addressing the ceiling-effect concern that weak binders can achieve spuriously high RRS values. The \SI{7.0}{\kcalmol} potency floor for Class A* ensures that only strong binders with uniform resilience across all mutants receive the highest classification.
    \item \textbf{African Chemical Space Index (ACSI):} A quantitative measure calibrated to the ANPDB, revealing that \SI{23.5}{\percent} of top candidates retain strong African NP character (ACSI $>$ 0.70) with a mean ACSI of 0.617 across all parseable candidates.
    \item \textbf{Polypharmacology Network Score (PNS):} A network-aware metric weighting target engagement by composite centrality (degree, betweenness, closeness, eigenvector) in the \textit{P. falciparum} STRING interactome, enabling biologically motivated candidate prioritization beyond docking affinity alone.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Unified antimalarial design engine.} This paper constitutes the second tier of a three-tier African antimalarial design engine. The first tier (Paper~1, companion manuscript) uses a VAE-based generative framework with dual-filter consensus docking to explore African natural product chemical space and identify polypharmacological candidates; the Tartarus benchmark and STONED-SELFIES scaffold leap analysis independently validate its predictions. The third tier (a forthcoming study on quantum-topological representations) resolves the scaffold paradox revealed by the generative expansion through the Topological Fingerprint, Tensor Network Embedding, and Quantum Kernel Score, demonstrating that ring topology (H$_1$) is preserved while peripheral connectivity (H$_0$) diverges during generation. Together, the three studies constitute the most comprehensive AI-assisted antimalarial discovery framework targeting African malaria strains published to date, grounded in African natural product chemical heritage.

\noindent\textbf{Validation and reproducibility.} All MD protocols, MM-GBSA calculations, and scoring metrics are fully documented with open-source code. The GROMACS 2025.4 simulations use CHARMM36m/GAFF2 force fields with validated equilibration protocols (EM \textrightarrow{} NVT \textrightarrow{} NPT). Statistical robustness is ensured through bootstrap confidence intervals, Bonferroni-corrected hypothesis testing, and explicit power analysis ($\sim$\SI{72}{\percent} power at $\alpha = 0.008$ for $n = 20$). Trajectories and scoring outputs are deposited on Zenodo and GitHub under open-source licences.

\noindent\textbf{Scope fit.} The manuscript introduces methodological contributions in resistance-aware MD validation, chemical space indexing for natural products, and network-pharmacology-weighted scoring, all within the computational chemistry and molecular modeling scope of \textit{JCIM}. The work extends the standard docking-based virtual screening pipeline into a dynamic validation framework that accounts for protein flexibility and resistance mutations, addressing a critical gap in antimalarial computational discovery.
