\noindent Dear Prof.\ Merz,

We submit our manuscript \textbf{``Interpreting Docking-Derived Resistance-Retention Scores in Antimalarial Triage''} for consideration as an Article in \textit{JCIM}.

This work evaluates a docking-based resistance-aware prioritization strategy and uses targeted molecular dynamics as a separate structural follow-up. It does not assign a systems-level mechanism of action from docking alone. Seventeen Set-C candidates were ranked by a docking-derived resistance resilience score (RRS) across six PfDHFR and PfCRT resistance states, with chemical-space (ACSI) and network (PNS) scores kept as separate ranking dimensions. Four parent-study complexes that are not on the Set-C list were simulated for \qty{10}{\nano\second} each as a short pose-retention check. On the 12 candidates with complete two-target data the classes were one A*, one A, four B, five C, and one D; five PfCRT-only compounds are reported separately. Only PfCRT--214 gave an interpretable MM-GBSA estimate ($\qty{-18.25 \pm 0.40}{\kcalmol}$). An external docking panel (39 ligands, 312 records) averaged near RRS 100, with little mutant/wild-type contrast under the same protocol. A Tartarus cross-check showed no composite MPO--affinity association, consistent with treating those scores as separate ranking tools.

A 16-system Set-C MD pilot on two candidates tests whether short trajectories and MM-GBSA recover the docking-RRS pattern. It is a single-replicate structural stress test, is not used to assign RRS classes, and is not biological validation. Sensitivity checks explore candidate removal and score perturbation. Companion Paper~1 results provide upstream methods and chemical-space context only, not independent replication.

Code, source data, and analysis scripts are available on GitHub under an open-source licence; a Zenodo snapshot will be deposited at publication. Statistics are reported with effect sizes, permutation $p$-values, bootstrap intervals, and Bonferroni adjustment.
