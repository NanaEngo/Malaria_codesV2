% ABSTRACT
% ============================================================================

\begin{abstract}
Resistance-aware polypharmacology docking stratifies antimalarial candidates by predicted mutant retention, but whether docking-derived and molecular-dynamics-derived resistance scores measure the same quantity has not been tested. We computed a docking-derived resistance-retention score (RRS) on \num{136} PfDHFR and PfCRT wild-type and mutant systems for a \num{17}-candidate African-natural-product cohort. The primary analysis on \num{12} two-target candidates produced a five-class stratification (A*:~\num{1}, A:~\num{1}, B:~\num{4}, C:~\num{5}, D:~\num{1}) that separates predicted wild-type potency from predicted mutant retention; network score and RRS were weakly associated ($\rho=\num{-0.2098}$, adjusted $p=\num{1.0000}$), indicating that the three ranking dimensions capture distinct information. Short \qty{10}{\nano\second} MD on four non-overlapping parent-study complexes left only PfCRT--214 with a bound ligand and an interpretable MM-GBSA estimate ($\qty{-18.25 \pm 0.40}{\kcalmol}$). A \num{16}-system single-replicate pilot on two Set-C candidates did not recover the docking retention pattern; trajectory metrics and docking-RRS disagreed in direction for seven of eight mutant comparisons, demonstrating that docking-RRS and MD-RRS are non-equivalent estimands. An external \num{39}-ligand panel (\num{312} records) gave a mean RRS near \num{100}, confirming that docking-score retention is protocol-dependent and not a general property of the scoring function. Docking-based RRS stratifies candidates inside this computational panel, but the ranking is hypothesis-generating only; short MD stress tests probe a different estimand and cannot validate docking-derived retention classes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Drug resistance remains the principal threat to malaria control in Africa. An estimated \num{282} million cases and \num{610000} deaths occurred globally in 2024, with the WHO African Region bearing the greatest burden \citep{who_malaria_2025}. Partial resistance to artemisinin derivatives, mediated by mutations in \textit{Pfkelch13}, is now confirmed or suspected in at least eight African countries \citep{artemisinin_partial_resistance}, and declining efficacy of some partner drugs has been reported \citep{who_malaria_2025,global_resistance_landscape_2026}. Spatial-temporal modelling further indicates that the geographic footprint of \textit{pfk13} markers in Africa is expanding \citep{young2026artemisinin}, while genomic surveillance documents the co-occurrence of \textit{Pfcrt} and \textit{Pfdhfr} resistance variants across diverse transmission settings \citep{letebo2026surveillance,africa_resistance_2026,pfk13_resistance_2026}.

Polypharmacology is a design strategy for complex disease biology, and multi-target-directed ligands represent an important translational class \citep{Ryszkiewicz2026MTDL}. The rationale is evolutionary as well as pharmacological: under the single-drug--multi-target (SDMT) paradigm, simultaneous loss of activity at several relevant targets raises the mutational barrier to treatment failure in a way that combination therapy alone does not \citep{sdmt2026trends,sdmt_trends_parasitology_2026}. That proposition is conditional, however. A list of predicted protein contacts is not a mechanism-of-action assignment; causal network analyses require an explicit perturbation model, a defined biological background, and evidence that the predicted interactions propagate to a phenotype \citep{Trapotsi2022MoA}. Network-learning approaches to off-target prediction illustrate the value of graph context while underscoring that network inference remains distinct from experimental target engagement \citep{Sentinel2025Offtarget}. Natural products are attractive starting points because their stereochemical and scaffold complexity supports activity across multiple protein classes \citep{polypharmacology_malaria_2026,value_addition_african_np_2025}. African natural products provide a particularly relevant chemical-space context, with resources such as ANPDB enabling explicit comparison with approved-drug and synthetic reference spaces \citep{anpdb_2026}.

In a previous study, we used generative molecular methods and dual-filter consensus docking (AutoDock Vina~\citep{vina_2021} and DiffDock-L~\citep{diffdock_2023}) to generate a library of \num{65856} candidate molecules from 396 African natural products and 454 synthetic drugs \citep{temgoua2026chemrxiv}. We selected the present candidates against a panel spanning established resistance determinants and mechanistically distinct parasite processes: PfDHFR (dihydrofolate reductase, PDB 7F3Y) and PfCRT (chloroquine resistance transporter, PDB 6UKJ) represent clinically established resistance-associated contexts; PfATP4 (sodium efflux pump, PDB 9N10) probes ion homeostasis; and the 4GM2 entry represents PfClpR, not PfClpP, and is retained as a proteostasis-related parent-study system. This panel is deliberately a mechanistic cross-section rather than an exhaustive parasite targetome. It allows us to ask whether resistance-aware prioritization remains informative across distinct target classes, while avoiding the stronger claim that the selected compounds define a complete parasite-wide polypharmacology profile. PfCRT docking and MD were performed at physiological pH; results for this transporter are therefore conditional on that preparation and may differ under digestive-vacuole pH.

Rigid docking is efficient for ranking but treats the receptor and scoring landscape in a simplified manner. MD tests whether a docked pose remains compatible with protein flexibility, whereas endpoint methods such as MM-GBSA provide system-specific energetic summaries rather than calibrated thermodynamic observables \citep{gmx_mmpbsa_2021,wang2019_mmgbsa}. This distinction is especially important for resistance analysis: a docking RRS is a hypothesis about relative mutant sensitivity, not a measurement of a mutant free-energy difference. Network pharmacology provides a complementary prioritization layer, but centrality is a topological weighting heuristic rather than a causal measure of target essentiality, consistent with the growing interest in multi-target antimalarial design more broadly \citep{multi_target_antimalarials_2025}. We therefore treat these quantities separately: docking-derived RRS for mutant sensitivity, PNS for network-weighted target ranking, ACSI for chemical-space positioning, and targeted MD for post-docking structural scrutiny.

Workflows that combine a docking-derived retention metric, network pharmacology and short MM-GBSA endpoints are sometimes presented as joint evidence for resistance-resilient polypharmacology, but each layer measures a different estimand and has its own scope of inference. Treating them as independent evidence of mechanism, rather than as parallel ranking signals with non-overlapping failure modes, inflates the apparent epistemic reach of a single short MD pilot. We make this distinction explicit throughout.

The central question is therefore twofold: (i) whether resistance-aware polypharmacology docking produces a discriminant prioritization that separates candidates by their predicted retention across clinically relevant mutant panels, and (ii) whether short molecular dynamics simulations corroborate the docking-derived retention signal or refute it by revealing that the two approaches measure non-equivalent biophysical quantities. The first question addresses whether RRS-based stratification is informative enough to generate hypotheses for downstream experimental testing. The second addresses whether MD, despite its richer representation of protein flexibility, converges on the same compound ranking---or instead exposes a fundamental mismatch between static docking scores and dynamic binding behavior. The primary analysis used \num{12} candidates with eligible PfDHFR and PfCRT wild-type scores; five PfCRT-only candidates formed a coverage-limited sensitivity set. RRS was calculated from empirical Vina scores, whereas PNS and ACSI provided complementary ranking dimensions. Four parent-study wild-type complexes---chemically and numerically distinct from the 17 Set-C candidates---underwent \qty{10}{\nano\second} simulations as a methodological pose-retention check; a 16-system Set-C pilot provided the only trajectory-level comparison against docking-RRS for members of the prioritization cohort. Biological polypharmacology and resistance circumvention were not tested.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Study design}

The primary analysis is docking-score retention (RRS) for the 12 Set-C candidates that have eligible PfDHFR and PfCRT wild-type scores. The central estimand is a computational triage signal, not a biological resilience phenotype. We therefore distinguish the primary docking-RRS analysis from secondary structural-retention diagnostics, technical validation, and unobserved biological quantities. Three secondary analyses are reported separately: the five PfCRT-only candidates, a four-complex parent-study MD check, and a 16-system Set-C MD pilot. They address different coverage and sampling questions and are not combined with the primary RRS classes. The parent-study MD systems are not among the 17 Set-C candidates and do not validate the Set-C ranking.

\subsection{Candidate selection and filtering}

The Set-C cohort is a filtered prioritization cohort rather than a representative sample of the parent library. Class frequencies are therefore conditional on the stated MPO, SYBA, selectivity, multi-target, and scaffold-diversity filters and are not estimates for the full \num{65856}-molecule library.

Seventeen candidates were selected from the Set-C candidate panel generated from the library of \num{65856} molecules described previously \citep{temgoua2026chemrxiv}. After MPO $\geq$ \num{0.70}, SYBA $>$ \num{0}, and SI $>$ \num{10} filtering, \num{19913} primary leads remained; the present Set-C cohort is a further prioritization of that filtered set. The candidate-selection panel is distinct from the production-MD cohort. Three sequential filters were applied: (1) MPO score $\geq$ \num{0.70} (primary leads); (2) SYBA score $>$ \num{0} (synthesisable); (3) selectivity index SI $>$ \num{10} (selectively antiparasitic). The selection additionally required multi-target engagement of at least two of the four docking targets and scaffold diversity of no more than three compounds per Bemis--Murcko scaffold. The selection includes one of the previously named consensus leads (Ligand 87, PfDHFR). The four additional parent-study consensus leads (Ligands 201, 214, 438, 164) were not part of the 17-candidate docking set but were used to construct the wild-type complexes for the production MD check. The 164 system is identified as the 164-labelled PfClpR cohort; PDB 4GM2 is PfClpR rather than PfClpP, so this system is not structural validation of PfClpP. Five approved antimalarial drugs (artemisinin, pyrimethamine, chloroquine, lumefantrine, cipargamin) were included as reference controls for the docking analysis (\cref{tab:system_breakdown}).

\subsection{Homology modelling of resistance mutants}

Crystal structures of mutant targets were not available in the PDB. Homology models were generated with SWISS-MODEL using the wild-type structures as templates: PfDHFR (7F3Y) and PfCRT (6UKJ). PfATP4 and the 164-labelled parent-MD system were not part of the six-mutant homology-model panel; moreover, PDB 4GM2 is PfClpR rather than PfClpP. Model quality was assessed by QMEAN score, Global Model Quality Estimation (GMQE), Ramachandran analysis (MolProbity), and C$\alpha$ RMSD to the wild-type template. Models were retained only when QMEAN exceeded \num{-4.0}, GMQE exceeded \num{0.7}, Ramachandran favored residues exceeded \qty{95}{\percent}, and backbone RMSD was below \qty{1.5}{\angstrom}. All six mutant models (PfDHFR N51I, C59R, S108N, I164L; PfCRT K76T, K76A) met these thresholds and were retained as docking receptors; a summary is given in the Supporting Information (\cref{SM-tab:s_homology_qc}). K76A was included as a charge-neutralising isosteric perturbation of the canonical K76T substitution, allowing the contribution of the charged Lys76 anchor to be examined separately from the threonine substitution. Model scores are not experimental structures, and the lack of mutant crystal structures or replicate ensembles remains a source of uncertainty.

\subsection{Docking protocol assessment}

The docking protocol and V2-corrected grid parameters used here are identical to those established in the parent study \citep{temgoua2026chemrxiv}: AutoDock Vina 1.2.7~\citep{vina_2021} with exhaustiveness \num{64} against grids centered on the corrected V2 binding-site coordinates of PfDHFR (7F3Y), PfCRT (6UKJ~\citep{Kim2019PfCRT}), PfATP4 (9N10), and the historically labelled PfClpR/4GM2 entry (not PfClpP). As an independent boundary audit of these fixed boxes, P2Rank~\citep{Krivak2018P2Rank} pocket predictions were compared with the grid centers; the concordant PfCRT result and the frame caveats for the other receptors are presented in the Supporting Information (\cref{SM-fig:s4_p2rank_boxes}).

Parent-study enrichment benchmarks (V2 grids) are summarized here and tabulated in full in the Supporting Information (\cref{SM-tab:s1_docking_validation}). On the MMV Malaria Box consensus (Vina+DiffDock), ROC-AUC was \num{0.924} (PfDHFR), \num{0.971} (PfCRT), and \num{1.000} (PfATP4). In contrast, DEKOIS 2.0 enrichment for PfDHFR with Vina alone was null (ROC-AUC \num{0.450}, EF$_{\qty{5}{\percent}}=\num{0.00}$), indicating that score-based recovery of known actives is protocol- and library-dependent. Redocking was considered accurate when heavy-atom RMSD was $<$ \qty{2.0}{\angstrom} relative to the crystal pose; the MTX redock attempt was a clear failure (RMSD $\approx$ \qty{30}{\angstrom}). AutoDock Vina was used for all reported Set-C scores; GNINA yielded no usable score for this panel. Docking scores are empirical scoring approximations that are sensitive to the scoring function and protocol~\citep{ferreira2023docking,sadybekov2023computational,kittelson2026docking}; they should not be treated as binding free energies.

The composite MPO--affinity association was null and exploratory; complete correlations are provided in the Supporting Information (\cref{SM-tab:secondary_tartarus_calibration,SM-tab:secondary_tartarus_correlation}).

\subsection{MPO sensitivity and predicted ADMET profile}

MPO-weight sensitivity and single-source ADMET-AI predictions were used descriptively; the complete sensitivity and endpoint results are provided in the Supporting Information (\cref{SM-fig:s1_mpo_sensitivity,SM-tab:s3_admet}). These predictions were not experimentally validated.

\subsection{Resistance resilience score definition}\label{sec:rrs_formula}

All 17 candidates were docked against each wild-type and mutant structure using AutoDock Vina 1.2.7 with exhaustiveness \num{64}. The 136 systems were ranked with empirical Vina scores rather than calibrated binding free energies. Grid boxes were centered on the co-crystallized ligand position with \qty{25}{\angstrom} dimensions, matching the audited V2 configuration files.

For target $t$, let $s_{\mathrm{Vina},i,\mathrm{WT},t}$ and $s_{\mathrm{Vina},i,m,t}$ denote the signed Vina scores. Candidates with $|s_{\mathrm{Vina},i,\mathrm{WT},t}| < \qty{5.0}{\kcalmol}$ are excluded from that target's score-retention analysis. The target-specific Resistance Resilience Score (RRS) is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RRS}_{i,m,t} = \frac{|s_{\mathrm{Vina},i,m,t}|}{|s_{\mathrm{Vina},i,\mathrm{WT},t}|} \times 100.
\end{equation}
RRS is a relative docking-score-retention statistic, not a free-energy difference or biochemical resistance measurement. The available-target summary averages the defined target-specific values across eligible targets and mutant states. To avoid letting unequal target coverage drive the main ranking, the primary analysis is restricted to the 12 candidates with eligible PfDHFR and PfCRT wild-type scores; the five PfCRT-only candidates are reported separately.

The mutually exclusive classes are defined from the available mutant values; primary class counts use the complete two-target subset:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Class A*}: all available mutant RRS values are at least \qty{80}{\percent} and the minimum eligible wild-type score magnitude is at least \qty{7.0}{\kcalmol}.
    \item \textbf{Class A}: all available mutant RRS values are at least \qty{80}{\percent}, but the minimum eligible wild-type score magnitude is below \qty{7.0}{\kcalmol}.
    \item \textbf{Class B}: all available mutant RRS values are at least \qty{70}{\percent}, but at least one is below \qty{80}{\percent}.
    \item \textbf{Class C}: at least one available mutant RRS value is at least \qty{80}{\percent}, but not all available values reach \qty{70}{\percent}.
    \item \textbf{Class D}: no available mutant RRS value reaches \qty{80}{\percent}.
\end{itemize}
The classes are mutually exclusive. The cut-offs follow the distribution of Vina scores in this panel and have not been calibrated to biochemical resistance or clinical outcome. Sensitivity to alternative thresholds is reported in the Supporting Information (\cref{SM-tab:s11_rrs_threshold_sensitivity}).

\subsection{Secondary ranking descriptors}

ACSI places each candidate relative to approved-drug and African natural-product reference spaces; weight-sensitivity checks are in the Supporting Information (\cref{SM-tab:s4_acsi_sensitivity}). PNS is a network-weighted ranking score; PfCRT centrality was imputed, with sensitivity reported in the Supporting Information (\cref{SM-tab:s7_pns_sensitivity}). Associations among metrics were assessed by Spearman rank correlation with \num{100000} permutations, Bonferroni adjustment for three tests, and bootstrap intervals from \num{10000} resamples. At $n=\num{12}$ these correlations are descriptive and are not powered tests of independence.

\subsection{MD protocol}

We simulated all four parent-study wild-type systems for \qty{10}{\nano\second} with CHARMM36m~\citep{charmm36m_2017}/GAFF2~\citep{wang2004gaff} at \qty{310.15}{\kelvin} using GROMACS~\citep{gromacs_2015,lindahl_2024}. The 10-ns length is intended as a pose-retention and local-geometry check, not as a measurement of residence time or converged free energy~\citep{wei2024structure}. We analyzed the trajectories after removing periodic-boundary discontinuities. MDAnalysis~\citep{mdanalysis_2011} was used to calculate contacts, H-bonds, RMSF, and radius of gyration every \num{10} frames and RMSD on every frame.

MM-GBSA endpoint estimates were obtained from production trajectories using the GB$^{\text{OBC2}}$ model (igb=5) with \qty{0.150}{\molar} salt concentration, via the \texttt{gmx\_MMPBSA} v1.5.0.3~\citep{gmx_mmpbsa_2021} CHARMM36-to-AMBER conversion. These are endpoint enthalpic scores, not rigorous free energies, and their limitations for ranking dissimilar complexes are well documented~\citep{roux2024mmgbsa,genheden2015mmpbsa}. Predicting mutation effects via endpoint methods remains challenging, with best-case correlations around 0.44 even at 100~ns~\citep{yu2022mutation_mmgbsa}. Configurational entropy is not included. Uncertainty is reported as the standard error of the mean over stored snapshots (\num{101} for the interpretable PfCRT--214 system; \num{100} for Set-C pilot systems). Within-trajectory and propagated standard deviations are tabulated in the Supporting Information and are not replicate uncertainties.

The 16-system Set-C pilot used the same temperature and length, with ligand parameters from OpenFF 2.2.0 (AM1-BCC) rather than GAFF2. Absolute MM-GBSA values are therefore not compared between the two cohorts. Each mutant was run as a single replicate; trajectories were accepted for endpoint analysis only after geometric quality checks.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Primary docking-RRS classification}

\input{Table_RRS_Primary.tex}

Mean target-specific RRS was 75.1 for PfDHFR (12 candidates) and 86.2 for PfCRT (17 candidates); full target-wise summaries are in the Supporting Information (\cref{SM-tab:s12_rrs_by_target}). The difference is in docking-score retention between panels, not evidence of different biological resistance.

On the complete two-target set ($n=\num{12}$) the classes were A*:~\num{1}, A:~\num{1}, B:~\num{4}, C:~\num{5}, and D:~\num{1}. When all 17 candidates are classified on whatever targets are eligible, the counts become A*:~\num{5}, A:~\num{1}, B:~\num{5}, C:~\num{5}, and D:~\num{1}; the five PfCRT-only compounds cannot be compared on equal footing with the two-target set. Per-compound RRS profiles across all six mutants are shown in \cref{SM-fig:s2_rrs_radar}. \section{Discussion}

\subsection{Docking-derived triage rather than validated resilience}

RRS compares each mutant score with the corresponding wild-type score after excluding wild-type magnitudes below \qty{5.0}{\kcalmol}. It is a docking-derived prioritisation statistic, not a biochemical measurement of resistance or affinity. Class~A* combines a wild-type score magnitude of at least \qty{7.0}{\kcalmol} with RRS values of at least \qty{80}{\percent} across all tested mutants; Class~A applies the same \qty{80}{\percent} retention threshold with a weaker wild-type magnitude floor; Class~B requires all available mutants above \qty{70}{\percent} with at least one below \qty{80}{\percent}; Class~C requires at least one mutant above \qty{80}{\percent} but not all above \qty{70}{\percent}; and Class~D denotes that no available mutant reaches \qty{80}{\percent}. The classes are mutually exclusive, combinatorially defined, and not evidence of resistance circumvention. They can be aligned with regional resistance surveillance in a future decision analysis, but they do not justify clinical deployment or therapeutic recommendations on their own \citep{wwarn_2026}.

This distinction is relevant in the current epidemiological context. The 2025 World Malaria Report documents declining efficacy of some artemisinin partner drugs alongside the spread of \textit{Pfkelch13}-mediated partial resistance, with both trends concentrated in the African Region \citep{who_malaria_2025}. Resistance-aware prioritization is a useful design criterion, though not a guarantee of clinical relevance: compounds selected without an explicit mutant-sensitivity analysis are poorly matched to the resistance states that are relevant in a given parasite population, but computational scores alone cannot establish that mismatch or its therapeutic consequence.

The external docking panel (mean RRS $\approx\num{100}$) underscores the same boundary. Under identical scoring and mutation definitions, mutant and wild-type Vina scores remained nearly proportional on an independent ligand set. The DEKOIS 2.0 PfDHFR benchmark for this protocol gave an AUC of \num{0.45} (95\% CI \numrange{0.37}{0.53}), confirming that the Vina scoring function does not recover known actives above chance on this benchmark---a result consistent with the observation that the docking scores function as a within-panel ranking tool rather than a calibrated affinity predictor. Possible explanations include limited sensitivity of the Vina score to these particular mutations in that chemical space, insufficient structural perturbation captured by homology models, or genuine score retention for many of those ligands. The data cannot distinguish among these alternatives; they caution against treating RRS as a calibrated resistance predictor outside the primary cohort.

A same-pose cross-scoring sensitivity analysis addresses one of these alternatives. Rescoring the same \num{312} poses with the GNINA CNN affinity (post-processing only) reproduced the class-level picture: all \num{38} eligible ligands were class~A under both scoring layers, with no class discordance (\cref{SM-tab:s15_robustness_transfer}). The retention-not-gain pattern is therefore not an artefact of a single scoring function. The ligand-level rank correlation between layers was moderate ($\rho=\num{0.558}$) and per-mutant correlations were weak or null (e.g.\ C59R $-0.076$), so the consensus supports class-level retention but not per-mutant rank transfer across scoring functions. Bootstrap intervals (\num{1e4} resamples) bound the same conclusion on the primary cohort: the class-~A* fraction was \num{0.294} (\numrange{0.118}{0.529}) and the PfCRT per-mutant retention intervals excluded \num{100} (K76T \num{85.4}, \numrange{81.0}{90.4}; K76A \num{87.0}, \numrange{83.1}{90.9}), whereas the PfDHFR intervals were wide and overlapped \num{100} for I164L (\cref{SM-tab:s15_robustness_transfer}).

The relationship between RRS and molecular topology remains unresolved. The companion TDA analysis reported associations in separate overlapping cohorts \citep{temgoua2026quantum}; because those candidates share provenance with the present study, the estimates are not independent validation. Topological descriptors should therefore be tested prospectively in larger, matched panels with mutant-state free-energy or experimental measurements before being used as a design rule.

The PfCRT component of this picture is mechanistically plausible in light of long-timescale simulations of the chloroquine resistance transporter: \qty{130}{\micro\second} of atomistic MD across CQ-sensitive and CQ-resistant isoforms established that Lys76 physically obstructs the drug-binding site while serving as the electrostatic anchor for peptide substrates, with R371I and Q271E acting in concert with K76T to open drug access \citep{tanner2025pfcrt}. Our mutant-vs-wild-type endpoint contrasts are consistent with this charged-cavity picture, in which perturbing residue \num{76} reshapes the local electrostatics that gate both ligand retention and substrate specificity. This consistency is contextual support, not an independent validation of the present docking scores.

\subsection{Chemical and network context}

ACSI places candidates relative to approved-drug and African natural-product chemical space. The mean was \num{0.543}, and two of \num{17} candidates (\qty{11.8}{\percent}) exceeded \num{0.70}. PNS is a network-weighted rank; on the two-target set it associated only weakly with RRS ($\rho=\num{-0.2098}$, adjusted $p=\num{1.0000}$). At $n=\num{12}$ the correlations mainly describe how the ranks line up in this cohort; the wide bootstrap intervals confirm that the three ranking dimensions capture distinct information. ACSI, PNS, and RRS are complementary ranking tools, not independent measures of biology, and their meaning depends on the chosen reference spaces, network, and targets \citep{anpdb_2026,value_addition_african_np_2025,Sentinel2025Offtarget}.

\subsection{Role of the structural stress test}

Short MD on the parent-study complexes shows both the value and the limit of structural follow-up when the compounds are not the ranked Set-C set. Two of four systems kept a bound ligand; the 164-labelled PfClpR and PfDHFR--201 systems did not. Only PfCRT--214 passed the checks needed for MM-GBSA, giving $\qty{-18.25 \pm 0.40}{\kcalmol}$---a system-specific endpoint that cannot calibrate Set-C scores. As is standard for endpoint methods, that number is a within-protocol guide, not a converged free energy \citep{wang2019_mmgbsa,gmx_mmpbsa_2021}.

The Set-C pilot is the only trajectory comparison with docking-RRS for compounds that are actually on the prioritization list. Its central contribution is calibration of interpretation: it tests whether a static score-retention signal and short-timescale structural retention behave as interchangeable quantities, rather than attempting to validate either quantity as a biological endpoint. Mutant MM-GBSA did not recover the docking-predicted weakening, so whether RRS tracks mutant-state free-energy differences remains unresolved. With one replicate and 10~ns per system, the pilot addresses local geometry and endpoint consistency, not residence time or experimental resistance. The pilot-scope retention gate illustrates the same boundary at class level: PP-01, the only candidate with a docking wild-type anchor on both targets, satisfied the two-target gate (docking RRS $\geq$ \qty{80}{\percent} together with pilot MD-RRS below parity), whereas the directional mismatch observed in most mutant--wild-type pairs persists. Because the pilot bound fraction is saturated at \qty{100}{\percent} for all sixteen systems, the gate is a coarse consistency check, not an affinity-equality proof.

\subsection{Non-equivalence of docking-RRS and MD-RRS estimands}

The central question of this study asked whether docking-RRS and MD-RRS measure the same quantity. The answer is clearly negative: seven of eight mutant comparisons showed directional disagreement. This non-equivalence is not a failure of either method but a consequence of what each estimates---docking-RRS reflects a static score ratio between mutant and wild-type poses, while MD-RRS captures dynamic binding persistence under explicit solvent and protein fluctuations. Failure-mode analyses of docking confirm that cross-target score comparisons under non-comparable states and over-reading scores as affinities are recurrent pitfalls~\citep{kittelson2026docking}. The two quantities diverge when conformational selection or induced-fit effects alter the binding mode upon mutation, which is precisely the regime relevant to drug resistance. For antimalarial lead prioritization, this means that docking-based RRS serves as a first-pass computational filter to stratify candidates, but it is not a validated prediction of resistance resilience. MD stress tests, even short ones, provide a complementary but non-redundant signal that probes a different aspect of the binding event. The practical consequence is that computational prioritization and structural scrutiny should be reported as parallel ranking dimensions with non-overlapping failure modes, not as convergent evidence of resistance resilience.

\textbf{What would constitute validation.}
None of the scores reported here is biochemical or biophysical evidence. The most direct test would be matched IC$_{50}$ measurements of the highest-RRS candidates on wild-type and mutant PfDHFR; PfCRT binding or transport assays at digestive-vacuole pH; and short growth-inhibition tests in culture to determine whether multi-target computational signals translate to a measurable phenotype. Agreement or disagreement with experiment would identify which computational signal, if any, is useful for each target. The multi-target, mutation-resilient candidates prioritised here are also natural inputs to machine-learning predictors of antimalarial combination synergy, in which the same molecular features would be scored for pairwise combination effects \citep{mlsypredc2024}; such synergy predictions remain outside the scope of the present study.

\subsection{Comparison with existing polypharmacology and resistance-aware approaches}

Polypharmacological drug design---the deliberate targeting of multiple biological pathways---has gained traction as a strategy to overcome resistance in infectious and complex diseases~\citep{hegedus2025polypharmacology,rafehi2024medicinal}. In malaria, multi-target approaches have been proposed to address the parasite's capacity for rapid resistance evolution~\citep{polypharmacology_malaria_2026,multi_target_antimalarials_2025}. Conventional multi-target docking ranks compounds by aggregated wild-type scores and does not treat mutant-state retention as a primary quantity. Network pharmacology often weights targets by centrality without comparing wild-type and resistance-associated structures under one scoring protocol~\citep{Sentinel2025Offtarget,abdelhamid2025polypharmacology_ai}. Under the SDMT frame, the present class-A* readout combines exactly the two axes such a strategy requires---target breadth in the four-target wild-type matrix and per-target mutational retention in the RRS panel---and is therefore reported as an SDMT-style hypothesis, not as demonstrated multi-target engagement~\citep{sdmt2026trends}. Free-energy methods such as FEP or TI can report mutant binding differences with clearer physical content, but they remain costly for early triage and need reliable structures and force fields. The approach used here sits between those extremes: RRS is an explicit mutant/wild-type docking-score ratio, while ACSI, PNS, and short MD remain separate layers rather than substitutes for one another. None of them replaces IC$_{50}$ panels, transporter assays, or converged free-energy calculations; they are intended only to shortlist compounds for those experiments~\citep{temgoua2026quantum,multi_target_antimalarials_2025,ghosh2025pfcrt_pfdhfr}.

\subsection{Limitations}

Mutant receptors were homology models. All six met the QMEAN, GMQE, Ramachandran, and RMSD thresholds, yet model quality is not experimental accuracy. MD was limited to 10~ns on four parent-study complexes and a single-replicate 16-system Set-C pilot; neither design addresses long residence times or convergence. The JCIM MD-reporting guidelines \citep{soares2023mdreport} recommend at least three replicates per system with adequate duration and convergence; no new replicate campaign was launched for the present submission, and the pilot is therefore reported strictly as a secondary protocol-level structural stress test rather than as a thermodynamic or kinetic measurement. An independent PP-01 PfCRT wild-type run differed from the reference endpoint by \qty{2.01}{\kcalmol}, which we take as a rough noise floor given snapshot autocorrelation (\cref{SM-tab:setc_wt_replicate_consistency}). Single-replicate mutant endpoints, including the Set-C MM-GBSA values, are sensitive to frame choice and periodic-boundary handling. MM-GBSA omits configurational entropy; only PfCRT--214 satisfied the bound-state and topology requirements. The four-target panel is not a full parasite targetome.

RRS class frequencies depend on the selection filters and on unequal target coverage; they should not be extrapolated to the full library. The cut-offs follow the distribution of Vina scores in this panel and have not been calibrated to biochemical resistance or clinical outcome (\cref{SM-tab:s11_rrs_threshold_sensitivity}). PfCRT network centrality was imputed, so PNS depends on how the network is built (\cref{SM-tab:s7_pns_sensitivity}). Network-pharmacology scores are generally sensitive to database coverage and hub bias \citep{panossian2025pitfalls}; the PNS reported here is therefore a bounded ranking heuristic, not a validated network property. Force fields differed between the parent-study and Set-C cohorts, so absolute MM-GBSA values are not compared across protocols. Cross-metric correlations rest on $n=12$ complete two-target candidates; the wide bootstrap intervals \textbf{confirm} that the three ranking dimensions capture distinct information rather than redundant signals. PfCRT was prepared at physiological pH rather than digestive-vacuole pH, which can change rankings \citep{temgoua2026chemrxiv}. Five candidates lacked an eligible PfDHFR wild-type score and appear only in the PfCRT-only analysis, a consequence of receptor setup rather than demonstrated selectivity.

\section{Conclusion}

Resistance-aware polypharmacology docking produces a discriminant prioritization signal inside the curated 17-candidate cohort: the 12 candidates with complete PfDHFR and PfCRT data fall into five mutually exclusive classes (A*–D) that separate predicted wild-type potency from predicted mutant retention (\cref{tab:rrs}). The three ranking dimensions---RRS, PNS, and ACSI---capture distinct information, with weak cross-correlations confirming their complementarity. Short molecular dynamics stress tests probe a different estimand and cannot serve as direct validation of docking-derived retention classes: on non-overlapping parent-study complexes, ligand retention occurred in only two of four systems; on the Set-C pilot, trajectory metrics and docking-RRS disagreed in direction for seven of eight mutant comparisons. Docking-RRS and MD-RRS are non-equivalent quantities; computational prioritization and structural scrutiny should be reported as parallel ranking dimensions with non-overlapping failure modes, not as convergent evidence of resistance resilience. An external docking panel averaged near RRS \num{100}, with little mutant/wild-type contrast under the same protocol. The ranking is useful inside this computational panel as a hypothesis-generating prioritization; it does not constitute a validated triage procedure and does not demonstrate biological polypharmacology, a systems-level mechanism, or experimental resistance circumvention. PP-01 \textbf{merits} experimental follow-up; PP-02 was not promoted by the pilot gate. Target-engagement assays, phenotypic tests, longer or free-energy simulations on mutants, and prospective experiments remain necessary before any biological claim can be advanced.
