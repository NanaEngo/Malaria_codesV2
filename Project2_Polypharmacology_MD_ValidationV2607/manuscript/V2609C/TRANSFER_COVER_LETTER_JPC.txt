Dear Editor:

I am writing to transfer our manuscript ci-2026-03544b, titled "Estimand 
Divergence Between Static Docking and Molecular Dynamics as a Triage Filter for 
Antimalarial Leads," from the Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling 
(JCIM) to The Journal of Physical Chemistry following the recommendation of the 
JCIM editorial team.


MANUSCRIPT OVERVIEW
-------------------

This work presents a systematic comparison of static molecular docking and 
explicit-solvent molecular dynamics simulations for prioritizing antimalarial 
lead compounds across clinically relevant resistance mutations. We report 
estimand divergence—directional disagreement in 87.5% (7/8) of matched mutant 
states—demonstrating that rigid-grid scoring and dynamic pocket accommodation 
measure complementary, non-equivalent quantities. The study evaluates 136 
protein–ligand systems spanning wild-type and mutant PfDHFR and PfCRT targets, 
with a 17-candidate cohort derived from 396 African natural products and 454 
synthetic antimalarials.


RELEVANCE TO THE JOURNAL OF PHYSICAL CHEMISTRY
-----------------------------------------------

Our manuscript is well-suited to The Journal of Physical Chemistry for the 
following reasons:

1. MOLECULAR DYNAMICS METHODOLOGY
   The study employs explicit-solvent MD simulations (GROMACS 2025.4, 
   CHARMM36m/OpenFF 2.2.0 force fields, 10 ns production trajectories per 
   system) with rigorous quality control, trajectory analysis, and MM-GBSA 
   endpoint free-energy estimates. This aligns with JPC's scope in 
   computational molecular biophysics.

2. STATISTICAL THERMODYNAMICS
   We address a fundamental question about the relationship between static 
   energy landscapes (rigid-receptor docking scores) and dynamic ensemble 
   averages (trajectory-based geometric retention), a core theme in physical 
   chemistry.

3. FORCE FIELD COMPARISON
   The manuscript documents a policy-approved force field substitution (OpenFF 
   2.2.0 AM1-BCC for small molecules instead of CGenFF/GAFF2), providing 
   methodological transparency relevant to the force field development 
   community.

4. IMPLICIT SOLVENT MODELS
   We employ the Generalized Born OBC2 model (igb=5) for MM-GBSA calculations 
   and explicitly justify the choice of GB over Poisson–Boltzmann in the 
   context of computational efficiency and sampling adequacy—a methodological 
   consideration central to JPC's readership.

5. PROTOCOL-DEPENDENT BEHAVIOR
   The manuscript quantifies scoring-function dependence through external 
   validation (DEKOIS 2.0 enrichment at chance level, ROC-AUC 0.450) and 
   CNN-based rescoring (GNINA), demonstrating that computational outcomes are 
   protocol-local calibration signals rather than universal physical 
   predictions.


KEY FINDINGS
------------

• Estimand divergence: Static docking predicted weaker mutant binding 
  (docking-RRS 83.9–94.6%), while 10-ns MD showed geometric retention above 
  wild-type reference (MD-RRS 106.4–133.9%) in 7/8 comparable states.

• External validation boundaries: Rigid ΔΔG calculations failed to reproduce 
  published experimental fold-shifts (direction agreement 28.6–41.7%, 24–28 
  compounds per genotype), and DEKOIS 2.0 benchmarking confirmed scoring-
  function limitations.

• Multi-metric independence: Docking-derived Resistance-Resilience Score (RRS), 
  Polypharmacological Network Score (PNS), and Antimalarial Chemical Space 
  Index (ACSI) showed weak pairwise correlations (Spearman ρ = -0.210 to 
  -0.531, adjusted p ≥ 0.233), confirming they capture distinct triage 
  dimensions.

• Honest negative results: We report null enrichment, directional mismatches, 
  and single-replicate limitations transparently, framing MD as a structural 
  stress test rather than a validated affinity predictor.


METHODOLOGICAL RIGOR AND TRANSPARENCY
--------------------------------------

The manuscript adheres to best practices for computational drug discovery 
reporting:

• PROVENANCE: All docking grids, force field parameters, MD protocols, and 
  analysis scripts are documented with version numbers and software citations.

• QUALITY CONTROL: Trajectory-level QC gates (RMSD, minimum distance, contact 
  count, H-bond occupancy) are predeclared; all 16 Set-C pilot systems passed 
  geometric acceptance criteria.

• STATISTICAL TREATMENT: Uncertainty propagation distinguishes within-trajectory 
  standard errors from replicate variability; Wilson confidence intervals 
  account for small sample sizes.

• SCOPE BOUNDARIES: The manuscript explicitly distinguishes computational 
  estimands (docking scores, trajectory-averaged geometry, endpoint MM-GBSA) 
  from unobserved biological quantities (experimental affinity, cellular 
  activity, resistance phenotype).


WHY THE TRANSFER IS APPROPRIATE
--------------------------------

While our original submission to JCIM emphasized computational triage workflows 
and multi-target polypharmacology scoring, the reviewers and editors recognized 
that the central contribution—quantifying estimand divergence between static 
and dynamic computational methods—aligns more closely with the physical 
chemistry foundations that underpin molecular simulation. The Journal of 
Physical Chemistry provides the ideal venue for a methods-comparison study 
grounded in statistical mechanics, force field physics, and the interpretation 
of free-energy approximations.
