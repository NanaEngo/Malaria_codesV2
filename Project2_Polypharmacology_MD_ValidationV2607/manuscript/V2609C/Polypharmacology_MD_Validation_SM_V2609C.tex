\noindent The 16-system single-replicate Set-C pilot on PP-01 and PP-02 tested whether short trajectories and MM-GBSA recover the docking-RRS pattern. All trajectories met geometric acceptance checks; trajectory metrics and docking-RRS showed opposite operational directions in seven of eight matched mutant comparisons. This result indicates that docking-RRS and MD-RRS are non-equivalent estimands under the tested protocols, rather than interchangeable measures of one underlying quantity. Scope boundaries are summarized in \cref{tab:s13_evidence_scope,tab:s14_panel_scope}.

\subsection*{Convergence and interaction analysis}
The canonical Set-C pilot comprises 16 MM--GBSA endpoints: 12 mutant systems and four WT baselines, each represented by one production trajectory and 100 stored snapshots. The displayed table reports the propagated within-trajectory standard deviation (SD$_{\mathrm{prop}}$). The SEM is a separate frame-level quantity and is not a replicate-level uncertainty estimate. A further PP-01 PfCRT WT replicate is a diagnostic control, not part of the 16-system Set-C table. The PP-01 PfCRT K76A diagnostic replicate is reported separately below because its endpoint differs substantially from the canonical endpoint.

The canonical Set-C endpoints have SD$_{\mathrm{prop}}$ values ranging from \qty{1.17}{--}\qty{3.62}{\kcalmol}; their frame-level SEM values range from \qty{0.16}{--}\qty{0.61}{\kcalmol}. Because snapshots are temporally autocorrelated and each state has a single production trajectory, neither quantity quantifies between-replicate uncertainty. The PP-01 PfCRT K76A difference of \qty{7.75}{\kcalmol} illustrates this limitation. The separate PP-01 PfCRT WT replicate showed a \qty{2.01}{\kcalmol} offset (\cref{tab:setc_wt_replicate_consistency}); this empirical offset is used only as a within-protocol endpoint diagnostic and is not applied to MD distance ratios.

Interaction fingerprints (ProLIF 2.2.1, \num{100} frames per trajectory, sixteen systems) identify a sparse set of persistent contacts: four to eleven contacts per system exceed \qty{50}{\percent} occupancy, most at \qty{100}{\percent}. Recurring anchors include PfCRT TYR16 (fully occupied in five of six PfCRT systems) and PfDHFR LEU46/MET55, consistent with the canonical binding pockets. As a feasibility probe, the single ligand PP-15 was docked to the wild-type states of PfCRT and PfDHFR (Vina 1.2.7) and prepared under the identical OpenFF~2.2.0/CHARMM36m explicit-solvent protocol used for the Set-C pilot. Both systems passed the pre-equilibration audits and completed EM--NVT--NPT equilibration and \qty{10}{ns} production trajectories with valid checkpoints (\cref{tab:s_pp15}); trajectory QC (heavy-atom protein--ligand minimum distance \num{< 5.0} \si{\angstrom} for \num{>= 10}\% of frames) passes for both, with a bound fraction of \num{1.000} (mean minimum distances \qty{2.77}{\angstrom} PfDHFR and \qty{3.20}{\angstrom} PfCRT). MM-GBSA estimates on PBC-whole trajectories (GB\textsuperscript{OBC2} igb=5, \qty{0.15}{\molar} salt) are \qty{-29.52 \pm 0.33}{\kcalmol} (PfDHFR) and \qty{-27.79 \pm 0.49}{\kcalmol} (PfCRT), and are reported in \cref{tab:s_pp15}. The probe is a Set-C RRS candidate but is outside the 16-system Set-C MD pilot: no mutant or additional RRS estimate is reported for this feasibility probe, and the single-replicate endpoints serve only as within-protocol feasibility contrasts. Docking-mode reproducibility was checked for PP-15 across five random seeds on the same grid box used for its canonical single-run score (\cref{tab:s17_multiseed_redock}); mode-1 docking score is stable to within \qty{0.03}{\kcalmol} on both wild-type receptors, so the seed choice does not drive the probe ranking. The same control was repeated for PP-01 on the canonical inputs of its wild-type scores (RRS-pilot ligand preparation and the upstream V2 wild-type grid boxes; \cref{tab:s17_multiseed_redock}): mode-1 docking score is stable to within \qty{0.05}{\kcalmol} on both wild-type receptors, and the canonical PfDHFR WT score (\qty{-7.50}{\kcalmol}) falls inside the five-seed spread (mean \qty{-7.499 \pm 0.021}{\kcalmol}). The canonical PfCRT WT score (\qty{-9.30}{\kcalmol}) lies \qty{0.04}{\kcalmol} above the observed range (\qty{-9.262}{--}\qty{-9.225}{\kcalmol}) because the original receptor-preparation detail of that single run is not preserved on disk; this offset is within the \qty{0.05}{\kcalmol} protocol-local seed-dispersion bound, so the seed-sensitivity conclusion is unchanged. Within the reproduced ligand, receptor, and grid configuration, the tested seed choices produced a dispersion no greater than \qty{0.05}{\kcalmol}. This supports protocol-local seed stability, but does not establish exact reproducibility of the historical PfCRT WT score because the original receptor preparation was not archived.
