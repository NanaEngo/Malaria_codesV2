\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Scenario B: Competitive but Not Superior}

Across all three evaluation scales — a \num{17}-molecule proof-of-concept
under LOO-CV, a \num{1000}-molecule P3-benchmark subsample under 5-fold CV,
and a \num{10000}-molecule external scaffold-split validation — the quantum
feature extractor (QFE) positioned consistently within the Scenario~B
envelope: competitive with the matched classical baseline but not
statistically superior. At P3-benchmark scale, the QFE
($0.8474 \pm 0.0129$) fell \qty{2.5}{\percent} below the matched
ECFP4-RBF SVM ($0.8693 \pm 0.0265$), and \qty{5.8}{\percent} below the
classical baseline under the more stringent scaffold split
($0.7831$ vs.\ $0.8413$). These results are consistent with the
honest-negative pattern established in Project~3\cite{sao2026p3}, where
quantum-inspired kernel methods ($\text{AUC} = 0.8385$) also fell short
of ECFP4-based classifiers. Crucially, QFE slightly outperformed QKS
(P3 quantum-inspired baseline, $0.8385$) at matched scale, suggesting
that direct structure encoding provides a marginal gain over classical-feature
quantum encoding — but neither crosses the ECFP4 threshold.

\subsection{Why ECFP4 Remains the Dominant Representation}

The ECFP4 fingerprint's persistence as the strongest representation across
this project series (P3, P5, and now P7) is consistent with an
information-theoretic argument: for binary antimalarial activity classification
derived from docking-score thresholding — itself a function of molecular shape
complementarity with a fixed protein binding pocket — the topological
neighbourhood structure encoded by radius-2 ECFP4 hashing is
precisely the information the task requires. Substructure identity, not
three-dimensional stereoelectronic detail, largely determines whether a
molecule docks favourably against PfDHFR, PfCRT, PfATP4, or PfClpP.

The QFE's encoding pipeline — ECFP4 $\to$ classical encoder $\to$ 4-qubit
parameterised circuit — is constrained by this same starting point: it
inherits ECFP4's topological representation and then projects it through
a low-dimensional quantum layer ($n = 4$ qubits, $2^4 = 16$-dimensional
Hilbert space). The quantum layer adds expressibility relative to a linear
projection but cannot inject stereochemical information absent from the
ECFP4 input. The BondOrderMatrix encoding route (encoding molecular structure
\emph{directly} into quantum states, as described in Boy et al.\cite{boy2025})
would genuinely test whether quantum encoding captures information ECFP4
discards; however, that approach requires per-molecule qubit counts
proportional to heavy-atom number (12--28 qubits for the P1 Set~A panel),
which is not tractable on statevector simulation for the P3-benchmark
($n \approx 19849$) or external scale. The QFE design represents a
pragmatic NISQ-era compromise, and its performance ceiling is bounded by
the classical encoder that precedes it.

\subsection{The Expressibility--Trainability Trade-off at Increasing Qubit Count}

The ablation study (4q, 6q, 8q; all depth~1, RZZ) revealed a non-monotonic
relationship between qubit count and performance: 4-qubit (AUC~$0.8474$)
$>$ 8-qubit ($0.8316$) $>$ 6-qubit ($0.8230$). The performance degradation
with wider circuits is consistent with the expressibility--trainability
trade-off documented in the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) and
variational quantum classifier literature\cite{tilly2022variational}: for
fixed circuit depth and fixed training-set size, wider circuits introduce
more parameters than can be reliably optimised, leading to flatter loss
landscapes and higher variance across folds. The lack of a barren
plateau signal at any qubit count (all mean $\|\nabla_\theta L\| \approx 0.293$)
indicates that gradient vanishing is not the cause; instead, the degradation
reflects insufficient training data per parameter rather than a
circuit-structure failure.

\subsection{Barren Plateau Risk at Training Scale}

The marginal barren-plateau signal observed at Phase~5 scale
(\qty{8.24}{\percent} of batches with $\|\nabla_\theta L\| < 10^{-6}$,
vs.\ \qty{0.0}{\percent} at $n = 800$) warrants attention as training scale
increases. The cause is most plausibly \emph{not} circuit-structure-induced
barren plateau (which would require exponentially vanishing gradients in
circuit width\cite{mcclean2018barren}) but rather input-induced near-zero
gradients: scaffold-novel test molecules with atypical fingerprint profiles
produce near-zero ECFP4-encoder activations, causing near-zero
quantum-layer inputs and near-zero losses for already-correctly-ranked
molecules. Mitigation strategies include adaptive batch sampling (oversampling
scaffold-novel molecules), larger hidden-dimension encoders, or circuit
expressibility augmentation through additional depth — all of which are
natural candidates for the Phase~6 Optuna hyperparameter optimisation
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\subsection{Scaffold Generalisation Gap}

Both ECFP4 and QFE degraded substantially when all test scaffolds were novel
($-0.108$ and $-0.064$ AUC points respectively). The smaller absolute
degradation for QFE does not indicate superior generalisation: starting from
a lower Phase~4 baseline, QFE had less room to degrade. The finding
replicates the scaffold-split pattern established in P3 and P5: antimalarial
activity prediction from docking-derived labels does not generalise well
across chemotype boundaries, consistent with activity cliff distributions
in the Malaria Box and related phenotypic screening datasets. Neither
classical nor quantum representations provide a generalisation pathway
to completely unseen scaffolds at the current training scales.

\subsection{Comparison with P3 and P5 Quantum-Inspired Benchmarks}

The QFE result fits coherently into the P3--P5--P7 trajectory
(Table~\ref{tab:summary}). Project~3 established that ECFP4 $\to$
IQPEmbedding (quantum-inspired kernel on \emph{classical} features) does
not exceed ECFP4 directly\cite{sao2026p3}. Project~5 showed that
GNNs and pretrained transformers also fail to surpass ECFP4 under
scaffold split\cite{sao2026p5}. P7 now extends this finding to
structure-informed hybrid quantum circuits: ECFP4-derived features, when
encoded through a quantum layer, achieve QKS-level or slightly above-QKS
performance, but do not surpass ECFP4 itself. The consistent pattern
across three distinct representation strategies — quantum-inspired kernels,
graph neural networks, and hybrid quantum-classical circuits — points to a
dataset-level boundary: the docking-score-derived activity labels used across
P3, P5, and P7 encode predominantly topological (substructure) information
that ECFP4 already captures near-optimally. A different activity label
source — experimental IC$_{50}$ or EC$_{50}$ — or a different task
(e.g., selectivity ratio prediction, which requires differentiation of
stereoisomers) may constitute a more discriminating test of quantum
advantage.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several limitations constrain the strength of conclusions that can be
drawn from this study.

\textbf{Activity label provenance.} Binary labels throughout P7 are derived
from docking-score thresholding, not experimental bioassay data. Docking
scores reflect molecular shape and electrostatic complementarity with
protein binding pockets rather than thermodynamic free energies of binding;
they are a useful proxy for prioritisation but not a validated surrogate
for biological activity.

\textbf{Small n at PoC scale.} The Phase~1 cohort of $n = 17$ is
severely underpowered for statistical inference. The LOO-CV result
(QFE $0.933 > $ ECFP4-MLP $0.833$) cannot be distinguished from
sampling variability at this size; it motivates Phase~4 scale-up but
does not constitute independent evidence.

\textbf{Simulation vs.\ hardware.} All quantum computations were performed
on PennyLane's \texttt{default.qubit} statevector simulator. Statevector
simulation is exact (no noise, no decoherence) and systematically optimistic
relative to NISQ hardware, where gate error rates of $10^{-2}$--$10^{-3}$
per two-qubit gate rapidly degrade circuit fidelity for the entangling layers
in the QFE. IBM Quantum hardware validation (Phase~6B) is required before
any hardware-realistic performance claim can be made.

\textbf{Encoder bottleneck.} The classical ECFP4 $\to$ 4-dimensional encoder
compresses a \num{2048}-bit fingerprint to \num{4} values; this compression
is constrained by the qubit count and may discard information relevant to
activity. Expanding the encoder (larger hidden dimension, additional layers)
at fixed qubit count is the most direct architectural improvement available
within the NISQ constraint.

\textbf{Subsample representativeness.} Phase~4 results are based on a
\num{1000}-molecule stratified subsample rather than the full
$n = \num{19849}$-molecule P3 benchmark. The expected $\approx \qty{8}{\percent}$
AUC degradation from subsampling (confirmed by comparing local ECFP4
$0.8693$ to canonical ECFP4 $0.9475$) means that Phase~4 QFE
($0.8474$) and Phase~4 ECFP4 ($0.8693$) are the correct apples-to-apples
comparison; the P3 canonical ECFP4 ($0.9475$) is a scale reference, not a
direct comparator.
