\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated hybrid quantum-classical feature extraction (QFE) for antimalarial
activity prediction across three evaluation scales using a consistent 4-qubit,
depth-1, RZZ-entangling architecture operating on ECFP4-compressed molecular
representations. The central finding is an honest-negative Scenario~B result:
QFE is competitive with the matched ECFP4-RBF SVM baseline but does not surpass
it at any scale tested (LOO-CV on $n = 17$; 5-fold CV on $n = 1000$; scaffold
split on $n = 10{,}000$). QFE AUC ranged from $0.8474 \pm 0.0129$ (Phase~4,
5-fold CV) to $0.7831$ (Phase~5, scaffold split), consistently above the
P3 quantum-inspired QKS baseline ($0.8385$) but $2.5$--$5.8$ percentage
points below ECFP4-RBF. Gradient diagnostics confirmed healthy training at
$n = 800$ scale (no barren plateau), with a marginal signal ($\qty{8.24}{\percent}$
of batches below $10^{-6}$) emerging at $n = 8000$, attributable to
input-induced near-zero activations on scaffold-novel molecules rather than
circuit-structure-induced gradient vanishing.

The qubit-count ablation (4q, 6q, 8q) identified 4-qubit as optimal on
the $n = 1000$ subsample: wider circuits degraded performance and variance
without reducing the expressibility--trainability gap, confirming that
deeper optimisation — not wider circuits — is the more productive
improvement direction.

Hyperparameter optimisation (Phase~6) confirmed that the manual ablation
finding is robust: a 50-trial Optuna TPE search jointly optimising circuit
width, depth, entangling gate, encoder dimension, learning rate, batch size,
and regularisation strength identified a 4-qubit, depth-1, CZ-entangling
configuration as best (2-fold inner-CV AUC $0.8487$). A canonical 5-fold
re-run of this configuration on the same frozen Phase~4 subsample yielded
AUC $= 0.8468 \pm 0.0209$, which is within noise of the Phase~4
RZZ canonical ($0.8474 \pm 0.0129$; $\Delta = -0.0006$). The predeclared
decision gate ($\geq 0.8574$) was not met; the Phase~4 4-qubit, depth-1,
RZZ configuration remains the canonical optimal. Deeper optimisation does
not close the gap with ECFP4-RBF, confirming that the performance ceiling
is imposed by the ECFP4-based encoder input rather than by circuit or
training configuration.

IBM Quantum hardware validation (Phase~6B) on the Phase~1 PoC cohort
($n = 17$) will quantify the gap between statevector simulation and
real-device performance for the 4-qubit circuit, providing the first
noise-realistic QFE benchmark in this series.

Beyond the immediate P7 scope, the consistent Scenario~B pattern across
P3 (quantum-inspired kernels), P5 (GNNs/transformers), and P7 (hybrid
quantum circuits) converges on a dataset-level interpretation: docking-score-
derived binary labels encode predominantly topological information that
ECFP4 already captures near-optimally for the four malaria drug targets
studied. Quantum advantage — if attainable for antimalarial activity prediction
— is more likely to emerge from experimental IC$_{50}$/EC$_{50}$ labels
that encode conformational, stereoelectronic, and solvation information
discarded by 2D fingerprints, or from structurally richer tasks such as
selectivity ratio prediction between resistance and wild-type variants, where
fine-grained 3D encoding provides non-redundant information relative to
topological fingerprints. BondOrderMatrix-based direct structure encoding,
the mechanistically motivated strategy from Boy et al.\cite{boy2025}, remains
the correct long-term direction; practical application at P3-benchmark scale
will require either fault-tolerant quantum hardware (beyond NISQ) or
efficient approximation methods such as Nystr\"{o}m kernel approximation
on hardware-native quantum feature maps.

This study confirms that the NISQ-era QFE, as implemented, provides a
meaningful and reproducible Scenario~B result: not quantum advantage, but not
quantum futility either. The approach is technically validated, gradient-stable
at moderate scale, and reproducibly benchmarked against the same classical
baselines used across the P1--P7 series.
