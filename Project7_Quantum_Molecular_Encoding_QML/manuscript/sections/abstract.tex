African natural products (ANPs) present a stereochemical encoding challenge
for classical molecular fingerprints: ECFP4 collapses stereoisomers into
identical bit patterns, discarding the three-dimensional information
central to ANP bioactivity. We investigated whether hybrid quantum-classical
feature extraction (QFE), encoding ECFP4-compressed molecular representations
into parameterised 4-qubit circuits, captures information that improves
antimalarial activity prediction relative to ECFP4-RBF support vector machines.
Experiments spanned three scales: a \num{17}-molecule African natural
product-derived proof-of-concept cohort under leave-one-out cross-validation,
a \num{1000}-molecule P3-benchmark subsample under 5-fold stratified
cross-validation, and a \num{10000}-molecule external scaffold-split validation
with \qty{100}{\percent} novel test scaffolds. Hyperparameter optimisation
via Optuna TPE sampling (50 trials, 3-fold inner CV) additionally confirmed
circuit configuration optimality. The central finding is a Scenario~B
honest-negative result: QFE is competitive with the matched ECFP4-RBF
baseline (AUC $0.8474 \pm 0.0129$ vs.\ $0.8693 \pm 0.0265$ at P3-benchmark
scale; $0.7831$ vs.\ $0.8413$ under scaffold split) and consistently
outperforms the P3 quantum-inspired kernel baseline ($\text{AUC} = 0.8385$),
but does not surpass ECFP4 at any scale. Gradient diagnostics confirmed
healthy training at moderate scale (no barren plateau at $n = 800$;
marginal signal at $n = 8000$). Hyperparameter optimisation confirmed the
4-qubit, depth-1 configuration as optimal (canonical re-run AUC
$0.8468 \pm 0.0209$; decision gate not met). The consistent Scenario~B
pattern across quantum-inspired (P3), GNN/Transformer (P5), and hybrid
quantum (P7) methods indicates that docking-score-derived binary labels
are near-optimally captured by topological fingerprints at current
dataset scales; quantum advantage is more plausibly accessible with
experimental activity labels or stereoelectronically richer prediction targets.
