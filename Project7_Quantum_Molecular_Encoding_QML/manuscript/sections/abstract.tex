African natural products (ANPs) present a persistent representation challenge
for computational antimalarial screening: ECFP4 fingerprints collapse
stereoisomers into identical bit vectors, and graph neural network message-passing
cannot distinguish cis/trans double bonds or polycyclic ring geometries across
novel scaffolds --- limitations documented across Projects~1, 3, and 5 of this
series. Quantum molecular structure encoding (QMSE), introduced by Boy et al.\
(\textit{Mach.\ Learn.: Sci.\ Technol.}\ \textbf{6}, 045076, 2025), encodes
bond orders, Z/E stereochemistry, and R/S chirality directly as one- and
two-qubit rotation angles in parameterised quantum circuits --- bypassing
fingerprint hashing entirely. We implement a hybrid quantum-classical model
coupling QMSE circuits (BondOrderMatrix and CoulombMatrix encodings,
PennyLane \texttt{default.qubit}) to a TensorFlow dense head, trained
end-to-end via automatic differentiation through both components,
and investigate whether this architecture can overcome the structural
encoding limitations for antimalarial activity prediction on the
19{,}849-molecule P3 benchmark.

We first establish that QMSE with full-molecule encoding correctly discriminates
all stereoisomer pairs tested: a Qiskit Aer statevector probe on five R/S pairs
(alanine, lactic acid, ibuprofen, naproxen, 2-bromobutane) yields
$K(R,S) = 0.010$ against $K(R,R) = 1.000$ ($\Delta K = 0.990$), and three
Z/E pairs (2-butene, 2-pentene, stilbene) yield $K(E,Z) = 0.983$ ($\Delta K =
0.017$). A critical architectural constraint is identified: the QMSE--TF hybrid
pipeline encodes molecules into an $n_\text{qubits} = 8$ register, which
captures only the first eight heavy atoms. Stereocenters at atom indices
$\geq 8$ --- the majority in drug-sized ANP molecules --- are outside
the encoding window and produce identical angle vectors for both enantiomers.

Under Optuna hyperparameter optimisation ($30$ trials, 3-fold inner CV,
$n = 1{,}000$), the best configuration (CoulombMatrix, 8~qubits, 2~layers,
IsingYY entangling, $h = 128 \times 3$, $\text{lr} = 3.2 \times 10^{-4}$)
is evaluated under canonical 5-fold cross-validation on the frozen
$n = 1{,}000$ P3 subsample (seed $= 42$, SHA-256 verified). The QMSE--TF hybrid
achieves AUC~$= 0.640 \pm 0.044$, a Scenario~C result:
$\Delta\text{AUC} = -0.229$ below the matched ECFP4-RBF SVM baseline
($0.869 \pm 0.027$; paired $t$-test $p = 0.0009$). The underperformance is
attributable to the qubit-count constraint (8-qubit truncation retains
$\leq 8$ atoms), harder optimisation relative to the 4-qubit ECFP4-VQC
configuration, and the quadratic cost of backpropagation through the quantum
layer. Gradient diagnostics confirm that gradient vanishing is not the
bottleneck: mean $\|\nabla_\theta L\| > 0$ across all folds.

The stereo probe result is the principal scientific contribution: QMSE
\emph{does} encode stereochemistry in principle, with kernel fidelity ratios
of $\approx 100:1$ between same-stereoisomer and cross-stereoisomer pairs.
The gap between encoding capability and predictive performance reflects the
qubit-count ceiling of current NISQ devices and statevector simulators, not
a fundamental limitation of the QMSE framework. A quantum kernel SVM on the
Aer simulator (Kernel Target Alignment $= 0.242$, P1 Set A, $n = 17$)
confirms non-trivial label alignment of the kernel matrix, supporting the
direction of the approach. Hardware validation on IBM Quantum devices and
full-molecule kernel evaluation at P3-benchmark scale remain open tasks.
