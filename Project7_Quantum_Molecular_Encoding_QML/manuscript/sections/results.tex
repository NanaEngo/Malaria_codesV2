\section{Results}

\subsection{Dataset Characterisation}

The proof-of-concept (PoC) cohort comprised \num{17} antimalarial candidates from
Project~1 Set~A (\num{2} active, \num{15} inactive; class imbalance 15:2). African
natural product (ANP) structural descriptors confirmed the expected chemotype
profile: mean $F_{sp^3} = 0.217$ (range $[0.000, 0.600]$), mean
ACSI~$= 0.213$ (range $[0.088, 0.371]$), and a total of \num{10} stereocenters
across the panel. ANP classification yielded \num{16} synthetic-analogue structures
and \num{1} simple phenolic; no macrocycles were present.

For the P3 benchmark scale experiments, the stratified subsample of
\num{1000} molecules drawn from the full \num{19849}-molecule dataset
(seed~$= 42$, SHA-256 frozen before execution) preserved the original class ratio
(\num{759} active, \num{241} inactive; 75.9\%). External scaffold-split validation
used a \num{10000}-molecule subsample of the \num{45943}-molecule external pool
(after exclusion of all P3 molecules), split 80/20 by Bemis--Murcko scaffold with
\qty{100.0}{\percent} scaffold novelty in the test set (\num{5130} unique scaffolds,
zero train--test scaffold overlap).

\subsection{Phase 1: Proof-of-Concept on P1 Set A (LOO-CV)}

results shown in Table~\ref{tab:phase1_loo}.

  $^a$ GIN AUC~$= 1.000$ on $n = 17$ reflects near-certain overfitting; interpret
  with extreme caution.
}
\end{tabular}

The QFE model (ECFP4-2048 $\to$ Linear-128 $\to$ Linear-4 $\to$ Tanh $\to$
$\times\pi$ $\to$ 4-qubit PennyLane circuit $\to$ Decoder-32-1) outperformed
the ECFP4-MLP baseline (AUC~$0.933$ vs.\ $0.833$; ratio~$1.12$) and passed
the predeclared decision gate ($\geq 0.80$). Gradient norms throughout training
were healthy (mean $\|\nabla_\theta L\| = 4.23$, range $[0.13, 22.4]$; barren
plateau: \textbf{NONE}), confirming gradient propagation through the hybrid
architecture. Total runtime on CPU statevector simulation was \qty{339}{\second}
for 17 LOO folds.

Statistical comparison on Brier score differences across LOO folds showed no
paired $t$-test significance (all $p > 0.05$; $n = 17$ is underpowered),
consistent with a small-effect, small-$n$ setting. Mann--Whitney $U$
(QFE vs.\ ECFP4, Brier) gave $p = 0.023$ uncorrected, not interpretable
as evidence of a real effect without multiplicity correction. The PoC result
therefore serves as a feasibility demonstration — establishing gradient
propagation and non-trivial ranking performance — not as a claim of quantum
advantage.

%% ── Phase 4: P3 benchmark (subsample n=1000, 5-fold CV) ────────────────────

\subsection{Phase 4: P3 Benchmark — 5-fold CV (Subsample $n = 1000$)}

\subsubsection{ECFP4-RBF Baseline}

On the matched \num{1000}-molecule subsample under 5-fold stratified CV,
the ECFP4-RBF SVM achieved AUC~$= 0.8693 \pm 0.0265$
(Table~\ref{tab:phase4_results}). The observed $\approx \qty{8}{\percent}$
degradation relative to the P3 full-dataset canonical value (AUC~$0.9475$)
is consistent with the reduced training-set size at subsampling scale and
confirms that the subsample is a fair, internally consistent comparison target.

\subsubsection{QFE 4-Qubit, Depth~1, RZZ (Canonical Configuration)}

The QFE model on the identical subsample achieved
AUC~$= 0.8474 \pm 0.0129$ (Table~\ref{tab:phase4_results}), placing it
\qty{2.5}{\percent} below the matched ECFP4 baseline (ratio $0.975$) and
\qty{5.8}{\percent} above the P3 quantum-inspired QKS baseline
($\text{AUC} = 0.8385$). Gradient diagnostics showed a mean norm of $0.293$
(range $[0.00105, 3.527]$) with \qty{0.0}{\percent} of batches below $10^{-6}$
— no barren plateau risk at this scale.

more parameters with the same circuit depth and identical training budget
($n_{\text{train}} \approx 800$) increase optimisation difficulty without
a compensating gain in Hilbert-space representational capacity.

\subsection{Phase 5: External Validation — Scaffold Split ($n = 10{,}000$)}

\subsubsection{Scaffold Split Setup}

The \num{10000}-molecule external subsample yielded a Bemis--Murcko scaffold
split with \num{5130} unique scaffolds and \qty{100.0}{\percent} scaffold
novelty in the test set, representing the most stringent generalisation test
available from the dataset (Table~\ref{tab:phase5}).

\subsubsection{Performance Under Complete Scaffold Novelty}

Both methods degraded relative to within-distribution CV when faced with
completely novel scaffolds (Table~\ref{tab:phase5}), a pattern consistent
with scaffold-split results across P3 and P5.

QFE degraded by 0.064 AUC points under scaffold novelty, compared to a
0.108-point drop for ECFP4 from its higher baseline. Despite this smaller

\subsubsection{Gradient Diagnostics at Scale}

At the $n = 8000$ training scale, gradient norms remained healthy overall
(mean $\|\nabla_\theta L\| = 0.261$) but \qty{8.24}{\percent} of training batches
recorded norms below $10^{-6}$, a marginal barren-plateau signal. This
contrasts with \qty{0.0}{\percent} at the Phase~4 ($n = 800$) scale, indicating
that the 4-qubit architecture is near the edge of its trainable range for this
problem size. The minimum recorded norm was $2.4 \times 10^{-10}$, consistent
with hard-to-classify scaffold-novel molecules generating near-zero loss gradients
during late-stage training.

%% ── Cross-phase summary ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Cross-Phase Performance Summary}

Across all three evaluation phases, the QFE model maintained an AUC within
\qtyrange{2.5}{7.4}{percentage points} of the matched classical ECFP4 baseline
(Table~\ref{tab:summary}), consistently outperforming the P3 quantum-inspired
QKS ($\text{AUC} = 0.8385$) under matched conditions, but not exceeding the
ECFP4 baseline at any scale. The pattern constitutes a Scenario~B
(competitive, not superior) honest-negative result across all phases.

The Phase~1 PoC result (QFE AUC~$0.933 >$ ECFP4-MLP $0.833$) should not be
interpreted as evidence of Scenario~A (quantum advantage): the $n = 17$,
LOO-CV setting is statistically underpowered, the GIN baseline achieved
AUC~$1.000$ indicative of overfitting, and the ECFP4 comparator was an MLP
rather than the stronger RBF-SVM. At P3-benchmark scale ($n = 1000$,
5-fold CV), the QFE is competitive with but does not surpass ECFP4-RBF.

%% ── Phase 6: Hyperparameter optimisation ────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Phase 6: Hyperparameter Optimisation (Optuna TPE)}

\subsubsection{Search Protocol}

To determine whether the manual ablation finding (4-qubit optimal) could be
improved by joint optimisation of circuit and training hyperparameters, a
50-trial Optuna TPE search was conducted on the same frozen Phase~4 subsample
(SHA-256 verified). The search space covered circuit width (\{4, 6, 8\}
qubits), depth (\{1, 2, 3\}), entangling gate (\{RZZ, CNOT, CZ, none\}),
encoder hidden dimension (\{64, 128, 256\}), dropout rate ($[0.0, 0.5]$),
learning rate ($[10^{-4}, 10^{-2}]$, log-uniform), batch size (\{8, 16, 32\}),
training epochs (\{60, 80, 100\}), weight decay ($[10^{-6}, 10^{-3}]$,
log-uniform), SVM $C$ ($[0.01, 100]$), and SVM $\gamma$ (\{scale, auto\}).
Inner evaluation used 3-fold stratified CV; a predeclared decision gate
required the best HPO AUC to exceed $0.8574$ ($= 0.8474 + 0.010$) to
warrant a canonical 5-fold re-run.

\subsubsection{HPO Result}

Among completed trials, the best HPO configuration (trial~7) achieved a
2-fold inner-CV AUC of $0.8487$ with parameters: $n_{\text{qubits}} = 4$,
depth~$= 1$, CZ entangling, $\text{hidden\_dim} = 64$,
$\text{lr} = 2.21 \times 10^{-3}$, $\text{batch\_size} = 32$,
$\text{epochs} = 80$ (Table~\ref{tab:hpo}). This configuration was
immediately re-run under canonical 5-fold CV (same subsample, same CV
seed, SHA-256 verified), yielding AUC $= 0.8468 \pm 0.0209$.

\subsubsection{Interpretation}

The decision gate was not met: the HPO canonical AUC ($0.8468 \pm 0.0209$)
is within noise of the Phase~4 manual ablation result ($0.8474 \pm 0.0129$),
with $\Delta = -0.0006$. The CZ entangling variant is statistically
equivalent to RZZ; the reduced hidden dimension ($64$ vs.\ $128$) provides
marginally better regularisation at this training scale without improving
AUC. These findings confirm that the Phase~4 4-qubit, depth-1 configuration
is robust across both manual ablation and automated hyperparameter search,
and that the ~2.5\% gap between QFE and ECFP4-RBF at $n = 1000$ scale
reflects a ceiling imposed by the ECFP4-based encoder rather than
suboptimal circuit or training configuration. Gradient diagnostics for
the canonical re-run showed no barren plateau (mean $\|\nabla_\theta L\|
= 0.248$; $0\%$ of batches below $10^{-6}$).
