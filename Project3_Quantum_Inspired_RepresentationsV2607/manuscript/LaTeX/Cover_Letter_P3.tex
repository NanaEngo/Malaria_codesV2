\opening{Dear Editors,}

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, \textbf{``Persistent homology resolves the scaffold paradox in AI-generated African antimalarial candidates: a topological and tensor-network fingerprinting study''}, for consideration as an \textbf{Original Research Article} in the \textit{Journal of Cheminformatics}.

This work introduces three novel molecular descriptors---Topological Fingerprint (TFP), Tensor Network Embedding (TNE), and Quantum Kernel Score (QKS)---benchmarked against classical fingerprints on 19,836 African NP-derived candidates. Persistent homology resolves the scaffold paradox in generative AI (92.6\% Tanimoto novelty with 69.3\% scaffold recovery), TNE achieves 6.1$\times$ real-atom compression on a standard workstation, and quantum kernels match classical baselines after proper hyperparameter tuning (QKS AUC 0.751 vs RBF 0.701, $p=0.088$, ns). We report honest negative results: on the corrected canonical benchmark, classical ECFP4 remains the strongest single descriptor for primary screening (AUC 0.948); the hybrid descriptor (TFP+TNE+QK) is provisionally reported (AUC 0.842) pending a reproducibility rerun of the original full-library run.

All data and code are deposited on Zenodo and GitHub under the MIT licence. This work is original, not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission.
