\noindent\textbf{Cover Letter — Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling}

\vspace{1em}
Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript entitled \textit{“Target-Anchored Computational Prioritization of African-Natural-Product-Inspired Antimalarial Candidates”} for consideration as a research article.

This manuscript addresses a specific methodological problem in computational antimalarial discovery: how to evaluate a fixed, polypharmacology-oriented cohort across structurally heterogeneous parasite targets without treating docking scores as measured affinity. We analyze \num{17} candidates selected before the corrected target-panel calculations and report a complete \num{17}$\times$\num{4} AutoDock Vina matrix for PfDHFR, PfCRT, PfClpP, and PfATP4. Each pocket is defined by target-specific structural evidence, and the manuscript distinguishes catalytic-site anchors, a membrane-associated proxy cavity, and a mechanism-motivated ATPase region without assigning them equivalent evidential status.

The main contribution is a reproducible pose-prioritization layer. All \num{68} candidate--target calculations passed a predeclared geometric gate based on rank-1 placement, anchor proximity, and box containment. We report target-wise score ranges and means, but deliberately do not average scores across unlike receptors. This design choice prevents an uncalibrated composite from being mistaken for a potency ranking. The corrected PfClpP analysis uses structure 2F6I; the historically used 4GM2 structure is explicitly excluded because it represents PfClpR rather than PfClpP.

The study is intentionally hypothesis-generating. The results do not claim experimental affinity, confirmed target engagement, resistance circumvention, or measured IC\textsubscript{50}/EC\textsubscript{50}. Instead, the pose matrix provides a reproducible starting point for structural analysis, biochemical testing, resistance-mutant assays, and candidate-specific molecular dynamics. The associated per-target resistance-resilience analysis is reported separately in the integrated study rather than inferred from wild-type docking alone.

The source code, candidate manifest, receptor and ligand files, raw poses, configurations, provenance records, and machine-readable tables are available in the project repository: \url{https://github.com/NanaEngo/Malaria_codesV2}. All authors have reviewed and approved the manuscript and declare no competing financial interest. This manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for considering our work.
