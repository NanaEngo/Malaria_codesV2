\noindent Dear Prof.\ Merz,

We submit our revised manuscript \textbf{``Computational Discovery of Antimalarial Candidates from African Natural Product Chemical Space''} for consideration as an Article in \textit{JCIM} (Manuscript ID: ci-2026-012015).

This work presents a validated generative scaffold-hopping framework for antimalarial discovery. Rather than a static hit list, the framework retrospectively predicts (retrodicts) known ground-truth antimalarials from the MMV Malaria Box before application to African chemical space, achieving \qty{69.8}{\percent} hit rates and consensus-score ROC-AUC \numrange{0.924}{1.000}. We demonstrate that \qty{92.6}{\percent} of VAE-generated molecules access chemotypes unreachable by local SELFIES perturbations of known drugs (mean max Tanimoto \num{0.206}), with a \num{1.84}$\times$ scaffold-to-whole-molecule Tanimoto ratio proving systematic ring-system preservation.

The three-component pipeline---scaffold-aware VAE sampling, dual-filter consensus docking (DiffDock + AutoDock Vina), and multi-parameter optimization---reduces screening costs by \qty{99.3}{\percent}. All validation protocols are documented with open-source code, and the full dataset is deposited on Zenodo (10.5281/zenodo.19608875).
