\section{Orthogonal LISH mechanism-of-action benchmark}
\label{sec:lish_si}

The molecular representations benchmarked in the main text (ECFP4, GIN, GIN--TFP, GIN--TNE, ChemBERTa \citep{chemberta_2020}) encode chemical structure. Whether those structural features capture pharmacological mechanism is tested here using the LISH mechanism-of-action (MoA) challenge \citep{lish_moa_2019}, which asks whether gene-expression and cell-viability readouts---phenotypic proxies of drug action---can predict MoA class. The public LISH release carries no versioned drug-to-SMILES mapping; the two benchmarks are structurally disjoint, and the LISH phenotype baseline cannot be compared to the molecular ROC-AUC figures in the main text. This section establishes an orthogonal, phenotype-only reference baseline --- not a ceiling --- for future structure-to-MoA work: it quantifies what unweighted phenotype features alone achieve on the same fold grid, without implying that molecular representations must surpass it to demonstrate pharmacological value.

\subsection{Data and preparation}

We drew on the public LISH MoA training release \citep{lish_moa_2019}, available from the challenge repository at \url{https://github.com/LISHarvard/moa_challenge}. After aggregating repeated assay observations to the drug level, the working table retained \num{772} gene-expression features, \num{100} cell-viability features, and three experimental-condition summaries.

The scored endpoint comprises \num{206} MoA labels across \num{3289} drugs \citep{Trapotsi2022MoA}. Multi-label annotations (each drug may carry several MoA labels) cause per-label classifier performance to vary; labels with insufficient test-set variation are excluded from the macro average on a per-fold basis. No versioned drug-identifier-to-SMILES mapping exists in the public release, so the data set could not be used to train or evaluate any molecular model.

\section{Calibration metrics for the molecular benchmark}
\label{sec:calibration_si}

Predictive accuracy (ROC-AUC) and predictive reliability (calibration) are distinct properties \citep{hanley1982rocauc,davis2006auprc,guo2017calibration,natoesci2017ece}: a model may rank compounds correctly yet assign poorly calibrated probabilities. Calibration metrics were computed from fold-level test predictions pooled across the extended campaign, yielding \num{99180} test records per configuration (five seeds $\times$ five folds, for each of four model families). ECE rises from $\approx\num{0.03}$ on the random split to \numrange{0.07}{0.13} on the scaffold and novel-scaffold partitions for every model family (\Cref{tab:s2_calibration}). ChemBERTa exhibits the largest degradation under scaffold shift (ECE \numrange{0.12}{0.13}), consistent with its greater sensitivity to structural distribution shift (\Cref{MAIN-tab:h1}). This degradation is the empirical motivation for the $\mathrm{ECE} \num{>0.08}$ recalibration guard proposed in the Distance-Aware Conformal Triage Filter (\cref{sec:triage_code_si}): when batch-level confidence breaches this threshold, post-hoc Platt scaling or isotonic regression is indicated before any hard-cutoff routing decision, on this panel and pending re-verification on any deployment distribution.

\section{Light GNN hyperparameter sensitivity and capacity defense sweep}
\label{sec:gnn_sensitivity_si}

A persistent objection to the GNN underperformance documented in the main text is that it may reflect suboptimal hyperparameter selection rather than an architectural constraint. A systematic GNN hyperparameter capacity sweep was executed on the canonical scaffold split with the same frozen \num{5}-fold $\times$ \num{5}-seed protocol. The base GIN architecture \citep{xu2019gin} was evaluated across hidden dimensions $h \in \{\num{64}, \num{128}, \num{256}\}$ and dropout rates $d \in \{\num{0.1}, \num{0.2}\}$, bracketing the canonical configuration ($h=\num{128}$, $d=\num{0.1}$) from both below and above. Each configuration produced \num{25} fold--seed records with finite AUC.

As shown in \Cref{tab:s3_gnn_sensitivity}, mean scaffold ROC-AUC remains strictly bounded between \numrange{0.8000}{0.8081}---a range of \num{0.0081} AUC points. The per-seed standard deviation (\numrange{0.0118}{0.0160}) exceeds the inter-configuration spread, indicating that performance variation is dominated by fold-level stochasticity, not capacity choices. All three configurations remain below the ECFP4--RF scaffold reference (\num{0.8300} $\pm$ \num{0.0023}); the gain from enlarging the GIN capacity is flat-to-negative, consistent with the honest-negative verdict. The extrapolation gap is therefore not explained by hyperparameter under-tuning within the swept range, and is consistent with an architectural interpretation --- although a capacity sweep cannot prove a 1-WL expressivity limit as the unique cause. The GIN--TFP cohort gains documented in the main text (\cref{MAIN-tab:structural_complexity}) support this interpretation from the opposite direction: injecting persistent-homology invariants ($H_0$, $H_1$) yields a positive gain in every structural cohort (\num{+0.0143} flat aromatics, \num{+0.0125} complex 3D, \num{+0.0102} polycyclics), an association reported descriptively and quantified with paired seed-level tests and molecule-level bootstrap intervals in \cref{sec:cohort_stats_si}.

\section{Nearest-training-neighbor Tanimoto decile stratification}
\label{sec:nn_deciles_si}

Test molecules were binned into \num{10} similarity deciles relative to their nearest training neighbour on ECFP4 fingerprints, as described in the main text (\cref{MAIN-sec:structural-complexity}). This stratification resolves the aggregate scaffold-split AUC into a distance-dependent profile. The structural paradox of the African NP panel---\qty{92.6}{\percent} whole-molecule novelty yet \qty{69.3}{\percent} conservation of privileged ring cores---manifests as a bimodal performance distribution: ECFP4--RF shows its largest margins in the cold extrapolation tail (D1--D4, NN-similarity \num{<0.40}), the regime where GNN performance on high-$Fsp^3$ polycyclic scaffolds is most consistent with 1-WL expressivity limitations, while all five arms converge to near-parity in the high-similarity core (D9--D10, NN-similarity \num{>0.53}). In the mid-range interpolation regime (D5--D8), topological fusion (GIN--TFP) partially recovers the deficit via persistent-homology invariants ($H_0$, $H_1$), closing the gap to $\Delta \in$ \numrange{0.001}{0.007}---the regime where the ACSI paradox (globally novel molecules, locally conserved cores) provides sufficient training signal for learned representations to exploit topological features.

Three regularities emerge from the decile-resolved profile (\Cref{tab:s5_nn_deciles}): (i) ECFP4--RF leads in every decile (D1--D10), localising the fingerprint advantage in the OOD tail---the regime where sparse bit-key encodings and message-passing architectures diverge most; (ii) the margin is non-monotone, collapsing from $\Delta = \num{0.053}$ at D1 to a minimum of $\Delta = \num{0.001}$ at D6 before widening again at D9--D10, reflecting the transition from extrapolation to interpolation and back to high-similarity convergence; (iii) ChemBERTa tracks the GNN family in mid-range interpolation (D5--D8) but degrades in the OOD tail (D1--D4), consistent with its calibration degradation (\cref{sec:calibration_si}) and scaffold-split AUC deficit ($\Delta = \num{-0.043}$, \Cref{MAIN-tab:h1}).

\section{Butina cluster split benchmark details}
\label{sec:butina_si}

The canonical Bemis--Murcko scaffold split assigns individual ring cores to train/test partitions, but structurally similar scaffolds may leak across the boundary via shared molecular features, a failure mode documented across widely used molecular benchmarks by recent leakage-aware splitting audits \citep{joeres2025datasail,joeres2026datasailaddendum}. The Butina cluster split \citep{butina1999} reduces this within-scaffold similarity overlap at the chosen cutoff: Murcko scaffolds are hierarchically clustered at Tanimoto distance cutoff \num{0.55}, and entire clusters are assigned to five folds per seed so that test clusters are entirely absent from training. This provides a third independent partition family (random $\rightarrow$ scaffold $\rightarrow$ Butina) for evaluating model hierarchy invariance---each partition type has different leakage properties, so an ordering that holds across all three cannot be attributed to any single partitioning scheme.

The model ordering observed under canonical scaffold splitting is preserved under Butina splitting (\Cref{tab:s6_butina}): ECFP4--RF (\num{0.8331}) dominates, GIN--TFP (\num{0.8232}) outperforms base GIN (\num{0.8202}), and ChemBERTa (\num{0.7776}) trails. This invariance across three partition families---random (ECFP4--RF \num{0.9433}, best), scaffold (ECFP4--RF \num{0.8300}, best), and Butina (ECFP4--RF \num{0.8331}, best)---indicates that the performance hierarchy is a structural property of the panel, not an artefact of evaluation design. The Butina split, which reduces within-cluster similarity overlap under the chosen cutoff, reproduces the same hierarchy, providing indirect evidence that residual scaffold-split similarity overlap \citep{bash2025umap} does not materially alter the model ordering on this panel.

\section{Conformal Triage Filter implementation}
\label{sec:triage_code_si}

The Python specification below states the decision rule of the Distance-Aware Conformal Triage Filter (main text, \cref{MAIN-eq:conformal_triage}). The filter routes each query molecule to a predictor based on nearest-neighbour Tanimoto similarity ($D_{\mathrm{NN}}$) to the training set. ECFP4--RF shows its largest margins in the cold-OOD tail (D1--D4, $D_{\mathrm{NN}}\num{<0.40}$), the regime where GNN performance on high-$Fsp^3$ polycyclic scaffolds is most consistent with 1-WL expressivity limitations; GIN--TFP persistent-homology projections improve GIN performance most in mid-range interpolation (D5--D8). Three routing branches correspond to the three structural regimes identified in the decile analysis (\cref{sec:nn_deciles_si}), with a calibration guard that raises a warning when an optional batch ECE value exceeds the stated threshold ($\mathrm{ECE} \num{>0.08}$ by default), matching the prose rule. This listing is a reproducible research specification of the decision rule rather than validated deployment software: its thresholds were informed by the calibration and decile analyses of the present panel, and no prospective virtual-screening precision gain is claimed or established here.

\begin{lstlisting}
import numpy as np
from rdkit import Chem
from rdkit.Chem import AllChem, DataStructs

def conformal_triage_filter(query_mol, train_fps, model_rf,
                            model_gin_tfp, ece_threshold=0.08,
                            batch_ece=None):
    """
    Distance-Aware Conformal Triage Filter: research specification of
    a distance- and calibration-aware routing rule for virtual
    screening of African natural product scaffolds.

    Routes each query molecule to a predictor based on
    nearest-neighbour Tanimoto similarity (d_nn) to the training set.
    ECFP4-RF is selected in the cold-OOD tail (D1-D4, d_nn < 0.40),
    where GNN performance is most consistent with 1-WL message-passing
    limitations on high-Fsp3 polycyclic scaffolds; GIN-TFP
    persistent-homology projections (H_0, H_1) are selected in
    mid-range interpolation (D5-D8). An optional batch-level ECE value
    (batch_ece) is compared against ece_threshold (default 0.08) to
    raise a calibration warning indicating that post-hoc recalibration
    (Platt scaling or isotonic regression) is indicated before ranking.

    Research specification: thresholds are informed by the calibration
    and decile analyses of the present panel; no prospective
    virtual-screening precision gain is established here.
    """
    # 1. Compute Nearest-Neighbor Tanimoto similarity (d_nn)
    #    D_NN(x) = max_{y in T} s_Tanimoto(x, y)
    q_fp = AllChem.GetMorganFingerprintAsBitVect(query_mol, 2, nBits=2048)
    sims = DataStructs.BulkTanimotoSimilarity(q_fp, train_fps)
    d_nn = max(sims)

    # 2. Decision routing based on structural distance
    if d_nn < 0.40:
        # Cold OOD Tail (D1-D4): route to ECFP4-RF (largest observed
        # ECFP4 margins on high-Fsp3 PNA scaffolds, Fsp3>=0.45, rings>=4)
        selected_model = "ECFP4-RF"
        pred_prob = model_rf.predict_proba(q_fp)
    elif 0.40 <= d_nn <= 0.60:
        # Mid-range Interpolation (D5-D8): route to GIN-TFP
        # (persistent-homology projections: H_0 connectivity + H_1
        # cycle persistence)
        selected_model = "GIN-TFP"
        pred_prob = model_gin_tfp.predict(query_mol)
    else:
        # High-Similarity Core (D9-D10): ensemble consensus
        selected_model = "Consensus-Ensemble"
        pred_prob = 0.5 * (model_rf.predict_proba(q_fp) +
                           model_gin_tfp.predict(query_mol))

    return {
        "selected_model": selected_model,
        "d_nn_similarity": d_nn,
        "predicted_activity_prob": pred_prob,
        "calibration_warning": (batch_ece is not None
                                and batch_ece > ece_threshold)
    }
\end{lstlisting}


\section{Structural-complexity cohorts: recomputed statistics and uncertainty}
\label{sec:cohort_stats_si}

The structural-complexity cohort values in the main text (\Cref{MAIN-tab:structural_complexity}) were recomputed (2026-09-17) directly from the archived canonical scaffold-split per-fold predictions under a predeclared per-seed estimand: for each seed, the seed's five disjoint fold-test sets are pooled (one prediction per molecule, labels verified against the frozen panel) and the cohort ROC-AUC is computed on that pool; the reported value is the mean $\pm$ SD over the five per-seed AUCs. The pipeline was validated by reproducing the whole-panel canonical scaffold anchors (ECFP4--RF \num{0.8300}, GIN \num{0.8047}, GIN--TFP \num{0.8138}) within the predeclared gate of $\pm$\num{0.01} (maximum deviation \num{0.008}). Earlier published cohort values lacked an archived producer script and numeric artifact and are superseded; the recomputation protocol, gates, and artifacts are documented in the P5 data-analysis report and operational log.

\Cref{tab:s6_cohort_stats} reports the per-cohort values, seed-level paired $t$-tests ($df=\num{4}$, two-sided) for GIN--TFP minus GIN, and 95\% molecule-level bootstrap intervals ($B=\num{2000}$, shared resample across arms and seeds). The GIN--TFP gain is replication-consistent (positive in 5/5 seeds on flat aromatics) but small; on the small polycyclic cohort ($n=\num{363}$) both the seed-level test and the bootstrap interval are inconclusive (the interval includes zero).
