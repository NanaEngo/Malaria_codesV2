Context & Motivation

1. Uncertainty Quantification in VAE Models

Challenge: Antimalarial drug discovery requires computational models that are not only accurate but
also reliable and uncertainty-aware. With over 247 million malaria cases annually and rising drug
resistance, false positives in virtual screening waste precious resources.

Architecture: Variational autoencoder with 3-layer 1D convolutions trained on SMILES representations

Algorithm Comparison: KMeans vs BIRCH vs Jarvis-Patrick

Model Comparison (32D vs 64D Latent Space):

Results:

⇒ Problem: Deep learning models for molecular design often lack explicit uncertainty quantification
⇒ Solution: Robust framework combining probabilistic VAE models with dual-filter consensus validation
How can we make deep learning for drug discovery more reliable through uncertainty
quantification?

Research Questions
1. Uncertainty Quantification: Can VAEs provide principled uncertainty estimates in chemical
space?
2. Robustness Through Consensus: Does combining physics-based and ML-based docking reduce
false positives?
3. Model Validation: How do we validate robustness across algorithms and external benchmarks?
4. Scalability: Can uncertainty-aware pipelines process large libraries (65,856 compounds) eﬀiciently?

Dataset & Properties
Source: 396 African natural products + 454 synthetic antimalarials, expanded to 65,856 molecules
Properties: 94.1% Lipinski-compliant, mean QED 0.590, 20,702 unique scaffolds (31.4% diversity)
Expansion Methods: Cheese API (similarity search) + STONED-SELFIES (structural mutations)

Reconstruction accuracy: 92.15% vs 93.23%
Validation loss: 53.01 vs 51.78
KL divergence: 6.43 vs 12.86
Training stability: Both stable
Key Finding: 64D model selected for higher reconstruction accuracy and explicit uncertainty through
KL divergence (12.86), enabling principled uncertainty estimation in chemical space

2. Robustness Through Consensus Validation
Dual-Filter Strategy: Physics-based (AutoDock Vina) + ML-based (DiffDock) docking
Results Across Four P. falciparum Targets:
PfDHFR (7F3Y): Correlation r=0.32, FP reduction 25%, ROC-AUC 1.000
PfCRT (6UKJ): Correlation r=0.18, FP reduction 38%, ROC-AUC 0.924
PfClpP (4GM2): Correlation r=0.41, FP reduction 12%, ROC-AUC 0.986
PfATP4 (9N10): Correlation r=0.27, FP reduction 31%, ROC-AUC 0.971
Key Finding: Low correlation (r = 0.1–0.4) confirms complementary information. Consensus reduces
false positives by 12–45%, providing robust predictions through method disagreement quantification

Validation Benchmarks:
DEKOIS 2.0 decoy set (PfDHFR): 40 actives, 1200 decoys
MMV Malaria Box: 399 confirmed antimalarial actives
Enrichment: EF5% = 1.78, BEDROC (α=20) = 0.521
Cross-Validation Results:
Perfect ROC-AUC (1.000) for PfDHFR demonstrates model reliability
High ROC-AUC (0.924–0.986) across all four targets
64D latent space superior to 32D in cross-validation
Sensitivity Analysis: MPO weight perturbations (±10–20%) yielded Spearman ρ = 0.26–0.97 for top1000 compounds. ADMET weights most sensitive.
