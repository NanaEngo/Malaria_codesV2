This Supplementary Information provides the extended scoring definitions,
additional candidate-level results, molecular-diversity analysis, scalar
benchmark details and algorithmic ablations that complement the main article.
The two analyses described in the manuscript are kept separate throughout:
the scalar benchmark uses the v12 public-activity reward, whereas the
candidate-level Pareto analysis uses the historical pre-activity vector of
MPO, SYBA, RRS-informed chemotype similarity and PNS-informed docking proxy.

% ============================================================================
\section*{S1\hspace{0.6em}Extended methods and scoring definitions}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Molecular generation.}
Molecules were constructed from a curated fragment vocabulary using a
scaffold-aware policy and a valence filter. The medium vocabulary was used for
the main four-method benchmark. Each episode began from benzene and allowed at
most \num{10} fragment additions. The retained MCTS configuration used
$c_{\mathrm{PUCT}}=\num{5.0}$, virtual loss $\nu=\num{0.01}$, progressive
widening $k=\num{1.0}$ and $\alpha=\num{0.5}$, and policy temperature
$T=\num{0.8}$. The policy combined fragment frequencies, scaffold similarity
and reactivity compatibility.

\paragraph{Pre-activity objective vector.}
For the historical Pareto analysis, each candidate was represented by
\[
  \mathbf{f}(s)=\bigl(f_{\mathrm{MPO}}(s),f_{\mathrm{SYBA}}(s),
  f_{\mathrm{RRS}}(s),f_{\mathrm{PNS}}(s)\bigr).
\]
MPO, SYBA, the RRS-informed chemotype proxy and the PNS-informed docking
proxy were maximised; SA was constant at \num{3.0} and was therefore not
used as an independent dominance dimension. The tree-selection value was a
scalar sum of the varying min--max-normalised components, while the complete
vector was retained for front maintenance and analysis.

The RRS-informed proxy was calculated from Morgan/Tanimoto similarity to the
P2 resistance-resilience reference chemotypes. The PNS-informed proxy was the
normalised aggregate of the three available Tartarus docking columns
(\texttt{score\_1syh}, \texttt{score\_6y2f} and \texttt{score\_4lde}). These
quantities are computational proxy dimensions: they are not direct WT/mutant
binding measurements and do not constitute a four-target biological assay.

The original Pareto records contained a constant SYBA fallback. SYBA was
therefore evaluated with the validated classifier before the displayed front
was re-derived; MPO, SA, RRS and PNS were unchanged. This preserves the
candidate discovery record while allowing accessibility to be displayed as a
non-constant supplementary dimension. The resulting front should consequently
be interpreted as a post-hoc re-derived front, not as evidence that informative
SYBA values guided the search.

\paragraph{Scalar benchmark.}
The v12 benchmark used a separate scalar reward with weights
$w_{\mathrm{MPO}}=\num{0.36}$, $w_{\mathrm{docking}}=\num{0.315}$,
$w_{\mathrm{SYBA}}=\num{0.135}$, $w_{\mathrm{SA}}=\num{0.09}$ and
$w_{\mathrm{activity}}=\num{0.10}$. RRS and PNS were not included in this
benchmark. Public-activity proximity was the maximum Morgan-2 Tanimoto to
experimentally active compounds in the independent ChEMBL IC$_{50}$/EC$_{50}$
panel (\num{22447} molecules, including \num{19321} active entries).

\section*{S3\hspace{0.6em}Molecular diversity}
% ============================================================================

For each generation method, the best molecule from each of the \num{20}
benchmark seeds was retained. ECFP4 Tanimoto dissimilarity and Bemis--Murcko
scaffold statistics were calculated with RDKit. The three stochastic methods
occupy distinct regions of chemical space, while greedy search is deterministic
under the present fragment vocabulary and returns one repeated local optimum.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering

  it returns the same local optimum for every seed.}

\end{figure}

\section*{S4\hspace{0.6em}Extended scalar benchmark}
% ============================================================================

The scalar benchmark comprised \num{20} independent seeds per method and a
fixed budget of \num{1000} search iterations. The values in
\Cref{tab:sm_benchmark} are the mean, standard deviation, observed range and
mean wall-clock time of the best scalar reward per seed.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Public-activity-augmented scalar benchmark across \num{20} seeds.
  Reward values are reported with the screened MCTS configuration; wall-clock
time is the mean per seed.}
  \label{tab:sm_benchmark}
  \begin{tabular}{lccccc}
    \toprule
    Method & Mean reward & SD & Minimum & Maximum & Mean time (s) \\
    \midrule
    Random        & \num{0.6724} & \num{0.0056} & \num{0.6616} & \num{0.6856} & \num{50.4} \\
    MCTS+ScafVAE  & \num{0.6649} & \num{0.0068} & \num{0.6554} & \num{0.6779} & \num{92.1} \\
    Greedy        & \num{0.4278} & \num{0.0000} & \num{0.4278} & \num{0.4278} & \num{1.9} \\
    Genetic algorithm & \num{0.6453} & \num{0.0124} & \num{0.6178} & \num{0.6715} & \num{2.0} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Random search has the highest mean scalar reward, followed by MCTS, the
genetic algorithm and greedy search. MCTS is therefore interpreted as an
objective-space explorer rather than as a universally superior scalar
optimiser. The difference between the scalar benchmark and the Pareto analysis
is intentional: the former tests single-reward performance, while the latter
retains candidate-level trade-offs.

\section*{S5\hspace{0.6em}Algorithmic ablations}
% ============================================================================

A factorial ablation evaluated the chemistry-informed policy, Pareto-front
maintenance, PUCT exploration, rollout temperature and fragment vocabulary.
The experiment used five replicates per factorial cell and a pre-activity oracle
configuration distinct from the v12 scalar benchmark; only within-ablation
effects are therefore compared. The marginal factor means are summarised in
\Cref{tab:sm_component_ablation}. The largest observed effects were associated
with the ScafVAE policy ($\Delta=+\num{0.148}$), Pareto-front maintenance
($\Delta=+\num{0.108}$) and the larger fragment vocabulary
($\Delta=+\num{0.079}$). Changing $c_{\mathrm{PUCT}}$ from \num{2.0} to
\num{1.0} improved the reward by $\Delta=+\num{0.055}$, whereas temperature
had a smaller effect.

A separate vocabulary comparison across \num{10} seeds per condition is
shown in \Cref{tab:sm_vocabulary_ablation}. It found a strong overall effect
(one-way ANOVA $F=\num{350.1}$, $p=\num{1.12e-26}$). The aromatic-only
vocabulary performed below the full, medium and minimal vocabularies; the
medium and minimal conditions differed modestly in the post-hoc comparison
($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=\num{0.0052}$), whereas the full and medium conditions did
not differ significantly.

The selection mechanism was also compared with a ParetoPUCT-style variant that
restricted each node to non-dominated children before applying the PUCT score.
At equal budget (\num{700} iterations and \num{5} seeds), the merged
hypervolumes were \num{15.56} for scalar-proxy selection and \num{15.49} for
Pareto-restricted selection. This comparison supports the interpretation that
front maintenance and policy-guided exploration, rather than a particular
vectorial selection rule, dominate the observed behaviour in this protocol.
