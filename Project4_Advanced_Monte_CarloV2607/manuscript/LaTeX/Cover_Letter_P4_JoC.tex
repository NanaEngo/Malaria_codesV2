\opening{Dear Editors,}

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, \textbf{``Pareto-guided Monte Carlo tree search explores multi-objective trade-offs missed by scalar antimalarial generation''}, for consideration as a Research Article in the \textit{Journal of Cheminformatics}.

The work addresses a recurring limitation of de novo molecular design: scalar reward aggregation that conceals trade-offs between potency, synthetic accessibility and drug-likeness. We introduce a Pareto-guided Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) agent that maintains a non-dominated front across multi-parameter optimisation (MPO), synthetic Bayesian accessibility (SYBA), resistance-resilience (RRS) and polypharmacology-network (PNS) scores, guided by a scaffold-aware fragment policy.

A four-method scalar benchmark under a fixed search budget and a public-activity-augmented reward yields a transparent ranking: random search achieves the highest mean scalar reward (\num{0.6724}), followed by MCTS at its screened optimal configuration (\num{0.6649}; mean gap \num{0.0075}, paired $t_{19}=\num{-4.97}$, $p=\num{0.0001}$), a genetic algorithm (\num{0.6453}) and deterministic greedy search (\num{0.4278}). MCTS is therefore not a single-objective optimiser and, under the \textbf{pre-activity} multi-objective protocol, returns a Pareto front of non-dominated candidates (four solutions, hypervolume \num{1.2366}) spanning the potency--accessibility trade-off, including a high-potency / low-accessibility extreme that scalar optimisation misses. Component ablations identify the chemistry-informed policy and the Pareto front itself as the dominant performance drivers.

We believe this transparent, methods-focused contribution aligns closely with the scope of the \textit{Journal of Cheminformatics}: rigorous benchmarking, public code and molecule sets, and honest reporting of both strengths and limitations of a multi-objective search strategy. All currently released data and code are available under the MIT licence in the public repository (\url{https://github.com/NanaEngo/Malaria_codesV2}). The Zenodo DOI \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19608875} is reserved; the upload remains pending and will be completed before final publication.

This work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission.
