\opening{Dear Editors,}

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, \textbf{``Pareto-guided Monte Carlo tree search reveals multi-objective trade-offs in antimalarial generation''}, for consideration as a Research Article in the \textit{Journal of Cheminformatics}.

The work addresses a recurring limitation of de novo molecular design: scalar reward aggregation can conceal trade-offs between potency, synthetic accessibility and resistance-aware multi-target objectives. We introduce a Pareto-guided Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) agent and evaluate it through two explicitly separated analyses. In the pre-activity search, an RRS-informed Morgan/Tanimoto chemotype proxy and a PNS-informed three-column Tartarus docking proxy are retained alongside MPO and SYBA. They are P2-informed computational proxies, not direct WT/mutant or four-target validation. The RRS/PNS signals were available during the historical candidate search; SYBA was constant at its fallback during that run and was recomputed explicitly before the reported front was re-derived.

A four-method scalar benchmark under a fixed search budget and a public-activity-augmented reward yields a transparent ranking; this benchmark intentionally disables RRS/PNS so that its scalar activity comparison is not conflated with the pre-activity domain-objective front: random search achieves the highest mean scalar reward (\num{0.6724}), followed by MCTS at its screened optimal configuration (\num{0.6649}; mean gap \num{0.0075}, paired $t_{19}=\num{-4.97}$, $p=\num{0.000085}$, 95\% CI $[\num{-0.0107},\,\num{-0.0043}]$), a genetic algorithm (\num{0.6453}) and deterministic greedy search (\num{0.4278}). MCTS is therefore not presented as a single-objective optimiser. Under the separate historical \textbf{pre-activity} protocol, followed by the documented SYBA re-derivation, it yields four non-dominated candidates (hypervolume \num{1.2366}) spanning a potency--accessibility trade-off, including a high-potency / low-accessibility extreme that fixed-weight scalar aggregation can discard. Component ablations show among the largest observed effects for the chemistry-informed policy and Pareto front; these effects are interpreted within that distinct ablation oracle configuration.

We believe this transparent, methods-focused contribution aligns closely with the scope of the \textit{Journal of Cheminformatics}: rigorous benchmarking, public code and molecule sets, and auditable integration of RRS-informed resistance and PNS-informed polypharmacology proxies into candidate search, together with an explicit post-hoc SYBA re-evaluation rather than an inflated claim of four-way optimisation. The paper also makes the limitation visible: the current front contains three PNS=1 candidates and RRS-informed proxy values spanning \num{0.141}--\num{0.223}. These results demonstrate auditable objective integration more strongly than broad phenotypic separation or direct biological validation. All currently released data, analysis scripts and molecule sets are available under the MIT licence in the public repository (\url{https://github.com/NanaEngo/Malaria_codesV2}) for reviewer inspection. The Zenodo DOI \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19608875} is reserved; the upload remains pending and is not presented as an already available archive.

This work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission.
