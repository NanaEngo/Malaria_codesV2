## Angles

### 1. [Point-of-view inversion — skeptical Q1 reviewer who wants to reject] — Target (a)
The "n=14" in ρ=0.916, p<0.0001, n=14 is not 14 paired observations of an H₁↔resilience relationship. The manuscript itself (L528) gives the class counts: Class A (resilient) n=3, Class D (vulnerable) n=1, with B+C=10 as "context." So the entire "resistance-resilient biomarker" claim rests on a contrast between **3 resilient compounds having higher H₁ persistence than 1 vulnerable compound** (3.74 ± 0.34 Å vs 1.73 Å, L513). A Spearman ρ computed over 14 points where the signal lives in 4 extreme points is a formality of where those 4 points sit, not a generalizable law; the 10 B/C points are along for the ride. A Q1 reviewer reads "biomarker, ρ=0.916" and asks: what is ρ when you remove the single Class D point, or when you compute it within Class A only (n=3)?
→ **What it opens:** the biomarker claim may collapse to a 3-vs-1 contrast; the correlation is a placement artifact of one outlier, not a dose-response over resilience. `[to verify]`

### 2. [Point-of-view inversion — D-GRIL/TopologyNet competitor] — Targets (b)+(a)
A competitor using differentiable/topological deep learning says: your **static, 4-statistic PersStats / 12-dim TFP is the wrong paradigm**. You reduce each persistence diagram to four summary statistics (count, entropy, max, mean persistence) and discard the diagram; D-GRIL learns a two-parameter filtration end-to-end and back-propagates through it. Your conclusion "H₁ is the mechanism that resolves the paradox" is contingent on which four statistics you pre-selected — a learned filtration could attribute the same variance to a cross-dimensional feature and dissolve the clean H₁-vs-H₀ dichotomy the paradox rests on. The authors half-admit this in the SM (L551): on summary statistics alone RF beats MLP, and TopologyNet/D-GRIL "operate on richer inputs (full persistence diagrams or multi-parameter persistence) where neural architectures may extract additional signal." If a richer PH representation re-explains the data without H₁ being special, the "H₁ preserved / H₀ diverges" resolution is a feature-engineering choice, not a topological truth.
→ **What it opens:** the paradox resolution may be an artifact of the chosen static summary; a differentiable PH model could re-explain the same library and demote H₁ from "mechanism" to "one of many correlated stats." `[speculative]`

### 3. [Point-of-view inversion — medicinal chemist] — Targets (a)+(d)
A medicinal chemist says: a topological descriptor of cycle rigidity tells me nothing about binding that I don't already get from size and ring count. H₁ persistence is a property of the molecular graph's cycle structure at a chosen filtration scale and is strongly collinear with **ring count (H₁ count mean 3.61), H₀ count (mean 38.05 ≈ atom count), molecular weight, Fsp³, and rotatable-bond deficit**. The "computationally inexpensive topological predictor of resistance tolerance" (L99/L524) is inexpensive precisely because it is a proxy for "macrocyclic, rigid, larger molecules" — which medicinal chemists already assume are more resilient. The manuscript never runs a head-to-head of H₁ persistence vs a non-topological rigidity proxy (Kier flexibility index, number of rotatable bonds, Fsp³, MW) against RRS. An unconsidered alternative explanation for ρ=0.916 is that RRS correlates with molecular size / ring count, and H₁ persistence rides that confound.
→ **What it opens:** H₁ persistence may be a re-labeled physicochemical prior (MW / ring count / Fsp³), not an independent topological biomarker; the novelty could be terminological, and partial-correlation controlling for size may erase the effect. `[to verify]`

### 4. [Nail test] — Target (b)
Is the "scaffold paradox" actually a paradox? The two statistics that define it measure different things **by construction**: 92.6% Tanimoto distinction is a whole-molecule fingerprint dissimilarity that is sensitive to substituents; 69.3% Bemis–Murcko scaffold recovery is a skeleton match that **strips substituents by design**. A library can trivially be substituent-diverse and scaffold-conserving — that is exactly what Bemis–Murcko decomposition guarantees. So there is no contradiction to "resolve." The "resolution" (H₀≈connectivity/substituents diverge, H₁≈rings preserved, L477–479) is then **circular**: H₀ and H₁ are just the TDA analogues of the two statistics that manufactured the "paradox" in the first place. The 1.84× ratio of scaffold-only Tanimoto (0.379) over whole-molecule Tanimoto (0.206, L479) is presented as confirmation, but it is the same decomposition re-expressed. Naming a definitional non-contradiction a "paradox" and then "resolving" it with the same decomposition inflates a tautology into a finding.
→ **What it opens:** the paradox is manufactured by comparing a substituent-sensitive metric with a substituent-insensitive one; the resolution renames that decomposition in TDA language, so "resolves the scaffold paradox" may be a rhetorical achievement, not a scientific one. `[speculative]`

### 5. [Nail test] ⚡ — Targets (a)+(b)+(c)
The authors' own SM (Table S9, L508) reports a topological descriptor that **matches/beats ECFP4**: PersStats (22 features across H₀, H₁, H₂) + RF = AUC **0.8731** vs ECFP4 0.868 (Cohen's d = +0.85, large positive), and TFP-12 + RF = **0.8668**. Yet the main manuscript headlines **TFP = 0.587** (L310, L440) and frames TDA as inferior to classical fingerprints — the foundational "negative result" that makes the "but H₁ is a resistance biomarker" pivot feel honest. Two possibilities, both damaging: (i) selective reporting — the 22-feature vector that includes H₀ and H₂ matches ECFP4, so the choice to headline the 12-feature variant (and the single H₁ stat for RRS) cherry-picks the weakest member of a feature family that works better when H₁ is NOT the sole star, undermining the "H₁ is the mechanism" story; (ii) an extraction bug — the manuscript already documents a PHCO SparseBitVect bug that depressed AUC from 0.500 to a corrected 0.801 (SM L232). The same class of feature-extraction error could be depressing the headlined TFP 0.587 while the SM's 0.867 is the correctly-extracted value. Either way the core double-message ("TDA loses at screening, but H₁ wins as a resistance biomarker") is contradicted by the authors' own supplementary table.
→ **What it opens:** if the 0.873/0.867 SM values are the honest TDA performance, the "TDA inferior" framing is a choice (or a bug), and the H₁-specific biomarker story is cherry-picked from a family where including H₀/H₂ works better — the paper's narrative arc inverts. `[to verify]`

### 6. [Nail test] — Target (c)
The "quantum kernel adds value for structurally heterogeneous / OOD regimes" claim (L378–379, L528) has **no OOD test behind it**. The only direct OOD-style benchmark the authors ran — the GA discriminator distinguishing generated from seed molecules (Table S6, L404–407) — shows the quantum kernel density at AUC **0.425–0.511** (near or below random) while classical ECFP4 Tanimoto scores **1.000**. The authors explain the failure away as "UMAP reduction discards resolution," then assert OOD value by citing the standalone QKS AUC 0.751 — which is an **in-distribution** binary classification task, not an OOD task. So every quantum-kernel failure is reframed as "not the right regime," and every quantum non-failure is reframed as "the right regime," with no actual OOD experiment to kill the claim. The "OOD value" framing is a retreat into a regime where no measurement was made.
→ **What it opens:** the OOD value claim is unfalsifiable as written; without an explicit heterogeneous/OOD benchmark, it is immune to the one test (GA discriminator) that was run and that the quantum kernel failed. `[speculative]`

### 7. [Scale change] — Target (a)
Does the H₁–RRS effect survive scale? The SM (L104, L639) confesses that expanding from the pilot n=14 to n=77 collapsed ρ from 0.947 (SM pilot) / 0.916 (main headline) to **0.361, p=0.001**; the prior audit (per review-plan S1) records a canonical **0.312, p=0.006, n=77**. A drop from ~0.92 to ~0.31–0.36 when n grows from 14 to 77 is not "attenuation" — it is a different finding. The main manuscript headlines the n=14 number in the abstract and conclusion and **buries the n=77 collapse in an SM figure caption**. At n=77, a ρ of 0.31–0.36 is in the range that a molecular-size or ring-count proxy (angle 3) would likely also produce, and the permutation/bootstrap stabilizers reported (p<0.0001, CI [0.799, 1.000], L639) are attached to the pilot, not the expansion. Over MD time trajectories (the authors' own planned follow-up, L524), a static single-conformer H₁ may fluctuate toward noise.
→ **What it opens:** the headlined biomarker is the small-sample version; the larger-sample version is a weak correlation that may be indistinguishable from a size confound, so "resistance-resilient biomarker (ρ=0.916)" is the optimistic tail. `[to verify]`

### 8. [Scale change] — Target (c)
Does the "quantum advantage on polypharmacology" survive correction or an external set? The Δ=+0.010 (σ=0.008) QKS-over-RBF polypharmacology gain (L365) has Δ/σ ≈ 1.25 — below the Bonferroni bar the authors apply elsewhere — and is reported as a 5-fold mean with **no corrected p-value and no held-out/external test**. The one external-style test the authors did run (the GA discriminator) returned a large **negative** ΔAUC (−0.57, Table S6). So the single positive quantum Δ is an uncorrected, in-sample, 5-fold mean, while the only quasi-external test of the quantum kernel showed it failing badly. On an external antimalarial library (ChEMBL/DrugBank), the +0.010 could plausibly reverse sign (as the GA Δ did) rather than persist.
→ **What it opens:** the polypharmacology "advantage" is one uncorrected small Δ; the only external-style test of the quantum kernel showed failure, so +0.010 is consistent with sampling noise that flips sign out-of-sample. `[to verify]`

### 9. [Nail test] — Targets (a)+(d)
The Discussion's mechanistic story (L515) — "rigid core locks the ligand, reduces conformational entropy penalty on binding, stabilizes the complex against active-site mutations → resilience" — is built on H₁ persistence computed from a **single ETKDG conformer (seed 42, L144)**. Static persistent homology on one frozen pose cannot distinguish a genuinely rigid macrocycle from a flexible molecule that happens to be frozen in a rigid-looking pose; **rigidity is a dynamic property the authors have not measured** (they defer it: "dynamic PH on full MD trajectories," L524). The causal chain rigidity→entropy→resilience is asserted from a static proxy of rigidity. Unconsidered alternative explanation: **both endpoints of the ρ=0.916 correlation are computational outputs** — H₁ from one conformer, and RRS from MD on docking scores (not experimental resistance data), with activity labels from the eos80ch ML model. So the "biomarker" is a correlation between two model outputs with no experimental anchor in between, and the rigidity mechanism is untestable without the dynamic PH the authors have not yet run.
→ **What it opens:** the resilience biomarker may be a correlation between two computational artifacts, and the rigidity mechanism rests on a static proxy of a dynamic property — so the causal story is a hypothesis about a hypothesis. `[to verify]`
