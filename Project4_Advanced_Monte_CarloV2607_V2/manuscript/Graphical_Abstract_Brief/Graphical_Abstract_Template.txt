SECTION 1 - THE BRIEF IN ONE PARAGRAPH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Build a three-panel graphical abstract for a computational chemistry paper that
reports an HONEST NEGATIVE RESULT. A Pareto-guided Monte Carlo tree search does
NOT beat random search on a single weighted reward. The paper's contribution is
that keeping a Pareto archive preserves chemically distinct design options that
a fixed weighting throws away, and that the paper quantifies what that costs.
The figure must make the negative result plainly visible while showing why the
method is still worth having. Any design that reads as "our method won" is
scientifically false and must not be produced.

  Method              Mean      SD        Bar order

Rules for this panel:
  - Random is the tallest bar and must be immediately legible as such.
  - Do NOT highlight, outline, saturate or otherwise privilege the MCTS bar.
  - Greedy's SD is exactly 0.0000 because it is deterministic. Show no error
    bar for it. Do not invent one. Do not omit the greedy bar.
  - Suggested y-axis: 0.40 to 0.70. Do NOT start the axis near 0.64 to make the
    Random-MCTS gap look dramatic, and do NOT start at 0 to make it vanish.
    The gap is small and real: state it numerically instead.
  - Annotate: "MCTS - Random = -0.0075 (95% CI -0.0107 to -0.0043), p = 0.000085"
    or an abbreviated form if space is tight.

Optional, only with spare room: a note that on the full 80-fragment vocabulary
the gap widens to -0.0214, with random ahead in 19 of 20 seeds
(data/benchmark_v12_allfrag.csv).

SECTION 5 - PANEL C: WHY IT MATTERS  (the payoff)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Source: data/pareto_front_4_candidates.csv and data/scalar_weight_sweep.csv

Plot the four retained candidates in the MPO (x) vs SYBA (y) plane:

  Label  SMILES                                        MPO     SYBA    seed
  -----  --------------------------------------------  ------  ------  ----
  P1     CNC(=O)c1ccc(OC(C)C(C)(C)C)c(OC)c1            0.910   1.000   13
  P2     COc1c(C)cc(-c2ccc(S(N)(=O)=O)cc2)cc1C         0.945   1.000   18
  P3     CC1CCC(n2cccn2)NC1(c1ccccc1)C1OCCO1           0.946   0.021   5
  P4     COc1cc(CO)c(O)c(C)c1C                         0.729   0.994   6

The story in this panel: P3 has the HIGHEST MPO of all four (0.946) but the
lowest SYBA (0.021). P2 is nearly as good on MPO (0.945) with SYBA 1.000. A
single fixed weighting picks P2 across 95.6% of the weight space and only ever
picks P3 when w_MPO exceeds about 0.997 - 0.3% of the space. The Pareto archive
is what keeps P3 visible at all. Make that legible.

Recommended: a horizontal weight-space bar beneath the scatter, segmented
  [ P2 : 0.0000-0.9556 | mid : 0.9556-0.9968 | P3 : 0.9968-1.0000 ]
with the P3 segment visibly tiny and labelled "0.3%".

Structure rendering: render from the SMILES strings above, unmodified. If only
two structures fit, use P2 and P3 - they carry the trade-off. Verify each
rendered structure round-trips to its source SMILES before delivery.

Do NOT plot SA or SA-inverse as an axis: it is constant (3.0) for all four
candidates and carries zero information.
